# Film and broadcast labels: what they ran, and how it maps to Label Man

October 22, 2026. Research and proposal only; no simulation behaviour changes here.
Follows [DemandAttentionImplementation.md](DemandAttentionImplementation.md), which
lists the film/broadcast second rank as the next item after the working-capital work,
and [DemandAttentionBoutiqueClosures.md](DemandAttentionBoutiqueClosures.md), which
asked for this research before any build. The working bundle is the `ttx-v1-tax`
bundle (`RunTaxTimingAudit.ps1` list plus `--prior-year-corporate-tax`).

## Summary

1. **Soundtracks are not where the film labels' missing share is.** Of the ten
   distinct soundtrack or cast-album #1 LPs in 1960–64, eight were on Big-5 labels.
   Elvis films and Mancini scores went to RCA through the performer's or composer's
   contract, and Columbia and RCA held the cast albums. Even United Artists' own
   *West Side Story* came out on Columbia. Only MGM (*Carnival!*, cast) and UA (*A Hard
   Day's Night*) were film labels. Label Man gives the Big-5 **100%** of charted
   soundtracks (46/46 and 39/39 in two 104-week seeds). That is too concentrated,
   but the correct share is still a large Big-5 majority.
2. **Their 12–21% of the Top 10 came from ordinary pop singles:** Connie Francis
   (MGM), the Everly Brothers (Warner), Paul Anka and Ray Charles (ABC-Paramount),
   Pat Boone (Dot), the Marcels and Shelley Fabares (Colpix), and Jay and the Americans
   (UA). Warner's #1 LPs were comedy and folk (Newhart, Sherman, Peter, Paul and Mary),
   not film.
3. **What made them different from both the Big-5 and the independents:**
   - **Parent capital and loss tolerance.** Warner lost about $3M a year over
     1958–62, cut staff from 100 to 30, and was kept open. MGM Records lost $18M in
     fiscal 1968–69. Colpix was closed in 1966 after its parent bought Aldon/Dimension.
   - **Independent distributors, not branch systems.** Warner's parent reportedly
     did not close it partly for fear of not collecting from slow-paying independent
     distributors. MGM executives in 1965 speak of choosing "the MGM distributors". These are
     the same wholesale channel the independents used. MGM had its own pressing plant
     (Bloomfield, NJ, 1947–72) and pressed for others, including Atlantic.
   - **Repertoire bought or leased, not only developed.** Warner paid the Everlys a
     reported $525,000 guarantee. Dot leased finished hits from independent producers
     (the Del-Vikings, the Surfaris). Berry Gordy placed early Motown acts at UA. ABC
     distributed Chancellor from 1957, and MGM distributed Cub, Hickory and later
     Kama Sutra. MGM bought Verve (December 1960); ABC bought Grand Award/Command (1959)
     and Westminster (1961).
   - **Media synergy, modest and specific.** Colpix and Warner recorded their own
     TV and film players (*The Donna Reed Show*'s Fabares and Petersen; *77 Sunset
     Strip*'s Edd Byrnes, #4). Colpix issued Hanna-Barbera TV albums and Columbia
     Pictures soundtracks (*Lawrence of Arabia*).
4. **Why the earlier head-template probe failed is consistent with this.** §17m of
   [HistoricalLabelTierBalanceResearch.md](HistoricalLabelTierBalanceResearch.md) gave
   MGM, ABC-Paramount and Warner Big-5 launch access (marketing, reach 0.75–1.0, 5–8
   national regions). Historically they had neither branch distribution nor the
   Big-5's launch machinery. What they had was money and bought or leased
   repertoire. Big-5 launch access let one template become a rank 1–3 firm,
   and the field paid for it.

## Proposed design (opt-in, not built)

One switch family, `--studio-parent-labels`, on top of the working bundle. It seeds
roster and parent economics only. It reserves no slots and scripts no records.

**Roster (1960).** MGM (+Cub), ABC-Paramount, Warner Bros., United Artists, Dot
(Paramount) and Colpix (existing template, re-parented). All MidTier at the **standard
MidTier launch envelope**, not the head envelope. Reach comes from contracted
independent distributors, as for any MidTier. No template may hold Major-tier status. Cub
and Chancellor remain their own labels, as distributed clients.

**Parent economics (the distinctive mechanism).** Each studio label has a parent with
an annual operating subsidy. The parent covers month-end deficits up to that budget,
so the label is not closed by the ordinary bankruptcy test while the parent is
willing. A parent review (annual) can close or sell the label after sustained losses
measured against the subsidy. Colpix in 1966 is the precedent. Warner shows that a
parent could carry several loss years. The subsidy size is a disclosed scenario value
scaled from Warner's ~$3M a year. No sourced figure exists for the others. Parent cash
never enters the label's own `cashReserves` for release judgement, so it
cannot buy Big-5 launch volume. It buys survival and acquisitions.

**Repertoire routes, all existing channels:**

1. *Master leasing.* Studio labels become preferred buyers in the existing national
   pickup route: they have the cash, and Dot and UA are the precedents. The chart slot
   goes to the studio label, but the producing label keeps the lease income.
2. *Free-agent bids.* Parent backing raises what a studio label can guarantee an
   experienced free agent (Everlys). This needs a check first: whether AI free-agent
   signing compares offers at all.
3. *Distribution clients.* No change. MidTier capacity already allows it.
4. *Soundtrack routing.* Give each `ExternalMediaProfile` a studio of origin
   (FilmScore and FilmSong only). Route in this order: performer or composer contract
   (if a contracted act fronts the film, that act's label), then the own studio label
   with first refusal at a sub-1 probability, then the open bid as now. StageCast
   stays an open bid, which the Big-5 cast-album houses win. Target shape: the Big-5
   keep a clear majority of blockbusters (8/10 historically) while studio labels hold
   a visible share of ordinary scores.

**Deferred.** TV-player signings wait for MediaAtmosphere to leave observe-only.
Late-decade consolidation (Reprise, Seven Arts, Atlantic, Transamerica/Liberty) stays
with the owner-Major path. Hot 100 label attribution
(`SimLogs/historical-hot100-1960-64` has no labels) is still owed and is the better
guard than the Top 10.

## Guards and prediction

§17m showed that, under a fixed 520 Top 10 slots, new competitive mass is paid for by
the field unless something acts on the Big-5's own launch entry. This design predicts a
smaller studio share than the head templates, because it adds money rather than launch
access. Where it adds share, much of it should arrive as leased independent
masters. The screen therefore reports chart share **two ways**: by selling label, and
by producing label (the master's originator).

| Guard | Pass | Kill |
|---|---|---|
| Studio labels' Top 10 share, 1960–62 | 10–20%, falling in 1963–64 | above 25% in any year; a studio label 1st in any seed-year; or a studio label in the top 3 in more than two of the five years of a seed (relaxed 2026-10-22 from "any top-3 place") |
| Big-5 Top 10 share | down, toward 22–26% in 1961–62 | up |
| Field share by **producing** label (revised 2026-10-22, below) | each year no further from the historical field share than the control is | below history by more than the tolerance, or further from history than the control by more than it |
| Labels with a Top 10 hit / first-time Top 10 labels | within seed noise | below 45 a year |
| Soundtrack licensees | Big-5 majority of blockbusters; studio labels 15–40% of charted soundtracks | Big-5 100% (no change) or below 50% |
| Studio-label closures | earned through the parent review only; logged | — |
| Small / Boutique / Independent closures, single units, Major sales share | within seed noise of `ttx-v1-tax` | outside it |

## Implemented: `--studio-parent-labels`

`Systems/StudioParentLabels.cs`, off by default. With the switch off nothing is created
and nothing reads the parent. It requires economic exits and the corporate model, and
it cannot be combined with `--seed-head-templates`.

- **Roster.** `AILabelFactory` appends MGM, ABC-Paramount, Warner Bros., United Artists
  and Dot (`label_0043`–`0047`) at the **standard MidTier** envelope, regions and
  opening cash. Colpix (`label_0017`, Independent) keeps its template and gains Columbia
  Pictures as its parent. `AILabel.studioParent` holds the parent. Studio labels cannot
  promote to Major, take a label P&D deal, or sell masters through `AiMasterDeals`.
- **Parent support.** Each month close, the parent accrues 1.25% of one month's market
  record gross (the sum of every label's last closed statement) into a 12-month window.
  If the label is below its commitment reserve, the parent tops it up to exactly that
  reserve, capped at the unused window. The anchors are Warner's ~$3M a year of losses
  on a ~$480M retail market (1960) and MGM's $18M over fiscal 1968–69. Both are about
  0.6% of retail, or about 1.25% of the factory value that the game's gross tracks. The
  game's 1960 gross is $198M against an unverified 1964 *Radio Annual* EIA/RIAA figure
  of about $228M factory and $480M retail. The contribution is a new cash kind,
  `ParentCapital`. It is excluded from pretax earnings and reported in
  `ownerTransfersNet`, so the solvency review and tax still see the label's own result.
- **Parent review.** At the December close, a year with any draw counts toward
  closure. After six consecutive such years the label closes with
  `exitReason=ParentClosure`. Warner was carried for five years.
- **Acquisitions.** The parent funds what the label's own free cash cannot cover:
  master shop, pickup and distress purchases, a soundtrack licence, or a free-agent
  guarantee. Studio buyers call first after own-sound fit, and their offer strength
  gets a +0.25 premium, which raises both their price and the pickup chance.
- **Contract-expiry bids.** Before a non-Major, non-studio incumbent decides on
  renewal, a studio label that the act can reach may bid for a proven act (a Top 40
  under this contract, or Established/Star/Superstar). The bid is twice the
  incumbent's renewal offer; a keyed draw gives an even chance of acceptance, and each
  label can win one bid a year. The act leaves on an ordinary `ContractExpired` and
  signs the bidder's term sheet with the guarantee as its advance.
- **Soundtracks.** Stage casts go to the open bid. A film draws a studio of origin
  (roughly even weights across the eight majors and "other"). Then a performer or
  composer contract sends 35% of ordinary scores and 60% of blockbusters to the open
  bid. Otherwise the studio's own label takes the album with probability 0.6. Universal
  maps to Decca from 1962 (MCA). Fox, Disney and others have no label here.

All coefficients are scenario values. Telemetry: `STUDIO_PARENT_LABEL`,
`STUDIO_PARENT_CAPITAL`, `STUDIO_PARENT_REVIEW`, `STUDIO_PARENT_CLOSURE`,
`STUDIO_FREE_AGENT_BID`, `STUDIO_SOUNDTRACK_ROUTE`. Analyzer:
`SimTools/analyze_studio_parent.py`.

**Verification (`spl-v1`).** `RunStudioParentAudit.ps1` ran nine native checks,
including `StudioParentLabelsChecks` (668 assertions), and a 13-week round trip with

**Reading.**

1. **The share is about a third to two-thirds of history's, and the shape is wrong.**
   History's film/broadcast labels were strongest in 1960 (21%) and faded. The model
   starts near 5% and peaks in 1963 (11.7%). The cold start is a roster gap: in 1960,
   MGM already had Connie Francis, Warner the Everly Brothers, and ABC-Paramount Paul
   Anka. The studio labels here open with ordinary MidTier rosters and must sign or buy
   their hits. The opening-careers seeding is the lever, not a stronger envelope.
2. **The field pays, but the field was too large.** The §17l guard ("field must not
   fall more than the Big-5") failed. In 1961, 1963 and 1964 the field by producing label
   fell 6–8 points while the Big-5 fell 0–3. Against history, though, the control's
   field is 13–28 points too high in every year, and the studio arm moves it toward
   history in every year (by 0.8–9.4 points). The guard was written for §17m, where the
   comparison was against an assumed-correct field. Here the historical comparison
   says some of the field's share belongs to the film/broadcast group. That
   reallocation is the point of the switch, not railroading. The independents' guard
   that matters is breadth, and it holds: labels with a hit stay at 52–61 a year. First-time
   labels fall in 1961 (−6), within about two SE.
3. **Most of the share is ordinary A&R, not the parent's money.** About two-thirds of
   studio Top 10 slots come from acts the studio labels signed themselves. Bids and
   leases supply the rest. The parent support never binds: losses at MidTier scale are
   tens of thousands of dollars a year, against a seven-figure budget. No label nears
   its review. Historically the money mattered because the firms were large; here it
   mainly guarantees that they survive.
4. **The Big-5 give up a little.** They are down 3.1 ± 0.8 points in 1962 (seed-consistent)
   and noisy elsewhere. The 1963 gap to history (25 vs 39) predates this switch.
5. **The guard set should be revisited.** The "no studio label in a top 3" kill
   triggered in two seed-years of 1963 (UA rank 1 with 41 slots in seed 1001, Warner
   rank 3 in seed 3003). Owner-grouped, history had MGM 2nd in 1960 (56 slots) and ABC-Paramount 3rd
   in 1962 (32); corrected 2026-10-22 from "MGM 3rd, ABC-Paramount 2nd". The guard was stricter than
   history, and UA's 41 is mostly one act's run
   (26 slots).
6. **Independents lose units but close less.** Independent unit share −5.2 points;
   Independent closures −4.5 points. Lease advances and royalties, and bid-funded
   departures that free roster costs, are plausible routes. This is not traced.
7. **Soundtracks are within the target.** 29% of film soundtracks go to their own
   studio's label; stage casts and contract-routed scores stay with the open bid, which
   the Big-5 win.

**Status.** Not adopted; the switch stays opt-in. It moves the Big-5 and the field
toward history but is too weak and too late on the film/broadcast share itself. Next,
in order:
(a) seed the studio labels' 1960 rosters through opening careers (Francis, the
Everlys, Anka-type established acts), then re-screen;
(b) replace the §17l field guard with the historical field share by year, keeping
the breadth guards;
(c) trace why Independent closures fall.

### (c) Why Independent closures fell in `spl-v1`: they did not, mechanically

`spl-v1`'s −4.5 ± 2.1 points sat entirely in **year one**. Independents closed by week 66 numbered
20 / 24 / 21 in the control and 12 / 10 / 11 in `spl-v1`. Almost all early closers are near-dead
labels with under $50k gross in year one (about 60% of that group closes in the control). P&D
deals, master leases and bids hardly touch that group. None of the studio labels' 7–13 year-one P&D
clients per world was low-gross, and at most one low-gross Independent per world signed any P&D deal. A diagnostic arm separated the five added firms from the
mechanics. `--studio-parent-labels-roster-only` creates the same five labels as ordinary MidTier firms
with no parent, stars, routing or bids (`spl-v2-roster`, same seeds).

| Arm | Independents closed, % (1001 / 2002 / 3003) | Closed by week 66 | Independent unit share, % |
|---|---|---|---|
| control `ttx-v1-tax` | 18.4 / 19.3 / 23.5 | 20 / 24 / 21 | 40.7 / 43.0 / 41.4 |
| roster-only | 18.0 / 17.5 / 21.8 | 15 / 11 / 22 | 36.0 / 37.9 / 40.3 |
| `spl-v1` | 16.4 / 16.5 / 14.8 | 12 / 10 / 11 | 36.8 / 36.4 / 36.3 |
| `spl-v2` | 16.4 / 25.5 / 21.8 | 10 / 19 / 24 | 31.9 / 36.4 / 35.9 |

`spl-v2` has the same world generation and the same mechanics as `spl-v1`; only eight acts' opening
histories differ. Even so, its seed-2002 and seed-3003 closures return to the control's level. On a
single seed the four arms span 9 points (seed 2002: 16.5–25.5). The `spl-v1` drop is reshuffle
variance in the most fragile group, and three seeds made it look consistent (see
`three-seed-paired-se-understates-reshuffle`). No mechanism produces it.

What is mechanical is the **unit share**. Five plain MidTier firms take 3.6 points of Independent units
(roster-only − control: −4.7 / −5.1 / −1.1). The studio mechanics and stars take another 3.3 (`spl-v2` −
roster-only: −4.1 / −1.5 / −4.4). That share loss does not show up as Independent closures, because
early closures are driven by labels that sell almost nothing either way.

**Status.** Still opt-in, not adopted. Remaining gaps: the 1960 level (7 against 21%) and the Big-5's
1960 overshoot. Deeper studio rosters were the proposed next lever; (d) below measures the star
allocation first and finds that more stars are not the gap.

### (d) Who holds the opening stars, against history

`OpeningCareerService.Stamp` now logs `OPENING_CAREER` for every seeded act: label, tier, studio flag,
archetype, rung, genre and #1 count. One-week runs (`oca-v1-control`, `oca-v1-studio`) reproduce the
265-week worlds; for example, the same MGM Superstar act is seeded in seed 1001. History has no rung
labels, so the comparison uses 1960 Top 10 acts: acts with **three or more** Top 10 records stand in for
Superstars (6 acts), and acts with **two or more** for the star band (19 acts). Groups are owner-grouped
as in (b). Means of three seeds:

| Group | History, 3+ / 2+ Top 10 records in 1960 | Seeded Superstars, control / studio arm | Seeded Superstars + Stars (30), control / studio arm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Big-5 | 2 / 7 (33% / 37%) | 3.0 / 1.3 | 13.0 (43%) / 6.7 (22%) |
| Studio labels | 2 / 6 (33% / 32%) | 0 / 2.7 | 0 / 9.0 (30%) |
| Field | 2 / 6 (33% / 32%) | 3.0 / 2.0 | 17.0 (57%) / 13.3 (44%) |

History's 6 are Brenda Lee and Elvis (Big-5), Connie Francis and Paul Anka (studio), and the Everlys
(still on Cadence for most of 1960) and Bobby Rydell (field). All of the field's seeded stars sit on
MidTier or Major labels, because the current-hitmaker and star archetypes admit only those tiers.
History's field stars were on Independents (Cadence, Cameo-Parkway).

**Allocation.** The studio arm already gives the studio labels about history's share of stars (30%
against 32%). Generic picks add two acts to the eight studio rows, because the studio labels' rosters
rank high on quality. The extra acts come out of the **Big-5**: their stars halve (13 → 6.7, against
history's 37% ≈ 11), while the field keeps more than history (44% against 32%). Deepening studio rosters
from the Big-5's budget would widen the overshoot measured in `spl-v2`. If anything, the studio rows
should come out of the field's MidTier picks.

**Conversion.** Stars barely become repeat hitmakers in either arm. From the 265-week runs, 1960 acts
with Top 10 records:

| | Acts with 2+ Top 10 records | 3+ | 2+ that are seeded opening acts |
|---|---|---|---|
| History | 19 (Big-5 7, studio 6, field 6) | 6 | — |
| Control `ttx-v1-tax` | 9.6 (Big-5 5.3, field 4.3) | 2.0 | 3.6 |
| `spl-v2` | 8.3 (Big-5 2.7, studio **0.3**, field 5.3) | 1.0 | 4.3 |

The studio labels hold nine seeded stars and produce 0.3 repeat Top 10 acts in 1960; their 7.7 Top 10
acts a year are mostly single hits. The 1960 studio gap (7% against 21%) comes from star-to-hit
conversion at MidTier scale, not from the number of stars. This is the established
`hit-acts-release-too-little` gap (0.44 against history's 1.17;
HistoricalLabelTierBalanceResearch notes Connie Francis's 4–5 Top 10 records a year). It is wider here
because a MidTier launch does less for a star than a Big-5 launch does. **Not deepening.** Two levers
follow, in order:
(1) take the studio rows from the field's MidTier picks instead of the Big-5's;
(2) the release cadence of hit acts, which belongs to the general hit-act work rather than to this
switch.

### (e) Rank guard relaxed to history's precedent

Owner-grouped, the best studio label ranked 2 / 7 / 3 / 5 / 7 in 1960–64 (MGM 56 slots in 1960,
ABC-Paramount 32 in 1962). None was ever 1st, and one held a top-3 place in two of the five years. The
analyzer's `rank_guard` (`studio-parent-rank-guard.csv`) kills a seed on any of:
- a studio label ranked 1st in any year;
- a studio label in the top 3 in more than two years;
- studio labels above 25% in any year.

| Seed | `spl-v1` best studio rank 1960–64 | Verdict | `spl-v2` best studio rank 1960–64 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1001 | 30, 27, 6, **1**, 12 | kill (1st 1963) | 10, 5, 17, 7, 15 | pass |
| 2002 | 14, 6, 9, 5, 10 | pass | 19, 2, 5, **1**, 2 | kill (1st 1963) |
| 3003 | 8, 6, 5, 3, 7 | pass | 7, 3, 3, 4, 3 | kill (top 3 in 3 years) |

Even relaxed, the guard kills `spl-v2` on two seeds. The pattern is the issue, not the count: history's
studio peak came in 1960, while the model's studio labels rise in 1961–64, as their acts build careers
and bids and leases add up.

### (f) `spl-v3`: studio rows taken from MidTier picks

`OpeningCareerService` changes, switch on only:
- Studio labels are left out of the generic archetype passes; their rows are their whole allocation.
- Each archetype pass selects its full count as before. Then `GiveUpMidTierPicks` demotes the
  lowest-quality MidTier Superstars and drops the lowest-quality MidTier picks, as many as the studio rows
  used. If no MidTier pick is left, the weakest pick anywhere goes instead. Log: `STUDIO_OPENING_GIVE_UP`.

Checks: switch-off seeding is unchanged (identical `OPENING_CAREER` lines, 95/95 CSVs in one-week runs), the
265-week switch-off replay is byte-identical to `ttx-v1-tax`, all nine checks pass, and the error logs are
empty. 23 of the 24 picks given up per world came from MidTier. The exception was one adult
standard-bearer in seed 3003, which came from a Big-5 label because no MidTier pick of that archetype existed.

**Allocation** (seeded Superstars + Stars, three-seed means). The right baseline is the roster-only world:

**Guards:**
- **Field by producer:** pass in every year, and it moves toward history in every year.
- **Breadth floor:** made relative to the control, because the control itself misses it. Seed 2002 has 39

- **First-time Top 10 labels:** within two SE in every year.
- **Rank:** seeds 1001 and 3003 pass. Seed 2002 fails, with a studio label in the top 3 in four years

**Reading.**
1. This is the best fit so far. The studio share has history's shape (12.7 in 1960, highest in 1960–62,

2. The Big-5 match history in 1961–62. They remain 4 points below it in 1960. Against the roster-only

3. Seed 2002 is a strong studio world (22% of 1960 slots) and fails the rank guard on frequency. Two of

4. Repeat-hit conversion is still the gap for every group: 9.7 acts with 2+ Top 10s against history's 19,
