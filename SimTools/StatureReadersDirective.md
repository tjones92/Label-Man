# Directive: emergent celebrity standing for AI acts

Status, 2026-11-04. Staged design for an opt-in AI progression screen. Initial evidence, calibration, and reader adoption have separate gates. References are `StatureReadersHandoff.md`, `CelebrityFanbaseDesign.md` §§6–7, and `VeteranChartInvestigation.md`. The reference profile includes the existing programme follow-up scheduling, delivery, and material slices.

## Objective and constraints

AI acts should become Rising, Established, Star, and Superstar because audiences increasingly seek them out and keep responding. A regional following, a run of strong sellers, an adult LP audience, or a television appearance that converts viewers into fans can start that progression. A Top 40 entry can accompany any of those routes. Weeks above a chart position, counts of hits, number ones, and scripted career events are excluded from the new advancement rule. The same evidence model should allow a late breakthrough, fading draw, and a comeback. Existing rank names are the presentation vocabulary.

In the current code, `SimulatedArtist.UpdateCareerState` advances the career ladder through chart achievements and also handles decline and some exits. `SeniorCooling.CoolOne` separately steps the ladder down using time since a Top 40. These are the legacy promotion and cooling writers to replace for participating AI acts. Signing, contract probation, drop, retirement, and disbandment are lifecycle decisions. During the pilot, derive standing alongside the saved `careerState` so those decisions continue to use their existing inputs. At cutover, expose the derived rank through the existing AI career display and migrate prestige reads to it. Keep lifecycle state explicit, especially where today’s enum value doubles as a contract status. The pilot covers AI-owned acts and AI decisions. Player progression and a new Major acquisition policy are outside its scope.

Media episodes are a separate axis. `FanEpisodeState` tracks local and regional attachment and feeds several live media readers through `EpisodeOf`. `SalienceState` in `MediaFollowing.Advance` tracks national youth sensations and cultural phenomena from smoothed attention share, breadth, and dominance. A durable adult Star can have neither national episode. A new act can briefly be a Sensation while its durable standing is still building. Both axes can arise from the same audience response, but an episode label, past peak, or rank must never be a required step or a direct input to the other axis. Keep both episode engines fixed during the first progression screen. If national episode readers later move from legacy fan episodes to salience, test that migration separately, with a reader inventory and explicit local/regional fallback. The current salience implementation measures sales, TV registration, and press. Live attendance is a possible future evidence family and needs its own coverage and calibration.

The veteran chart investigation found an excess of early-cohort Top 10 weeks and large follower-related launch-stock differences across all launches as well as among successful records. A completed one-seed same-build screen lowered the 1960 cohort's 1964 Top 10 share from 32.5% to 15.4% with follower demand off, but also reduced repeat Top 40 entry share from 55.3% to 41.8%. In 1968 the 1960–64 cohort's share was 63.3% on versus 31.3% off (history 27.7%), while repeat entry share fell from 56.0% to 44.1%. Album volume also changed. These diverging-world results do not identify stock as the cause. Following and recognition can magnify the same incumbent advantage that a stature reader would reward. Include cohort age, new acts and repeat hits in every causal decision; do not optimize concentration or Major share alone. The full follower-demand switch also controls purchase intent, live draw and programme audience reads, so its ablation cannot identify the effect of stock alone.

## Phase 0: inventory and measurement

### Weekly standing model

Use one deterministic update after the completed market week, once sales and audience registration are settled and `FollowingWeek` has updated the following. Publish a single dated snapshot for subsequent AI decisions. Missing releases are ordinary weeks. Advance the decay clock even when an act has no current record, and preserve the last processed week across save/load. Separate the following measurements in the snapshot.

| Measurement | Proposed input and role |
|---|---|
| Current audience response | Smoothed shares of realized single and LP sales within their own markets and audience segments. Include off-chart sales where available. Regional sales or attributable audience conversion can establish early traction. Use actual incremental viewer registration or fan joins as the TV response signal. Bookings, reach estimates, coverage, and spending describe opportunities to respond. |
| Durable draw | Existing young and adult followings, with their existing churn, evaluated against a lagged reference. Show format-specific expected advance intent as an interpretation of that following. Count a following and its derived advance orders once. Record recognition separately and test whether it adds information beyond realized response. |
| Reach | Regional distribution of realized response and durable following. Measure local strength relative to the reachable market, then grow the contribution toward national bands as it spreads across meaningful markets. Use population or market volume for weighting and a minimum denominator for sparse markets. A small local market can support Rising. National standing requires proportionate breadth or volume, whichever the observed distribution supports. |

Start with three observable routes. **Regional traction** reads response relative to a region and fades in influence for national bands. **Record draw** reads realized sales share by format, so adult LP success has a comparable route without needing a youth hit. **Audience conversion** reads registered or retained audience response and durable following. A large airing or press campaign contributes through the response it produces. Separate durable draw from recent response when computing each route and use the strongest supported route for the initial shadow candidate. A large one-week response can move the smoothed score quickly, while sustained smaller responses accumulate to the same band. Apply existing following churn before the read and smooth volatile weekly response once. Fit and publish smoothing rates and route scales from shadow traces. Compare against the handoff's chart-form/following/recognition maximum as a diagnostic, including which candidate better explains trajectories without repeatedly counting the same sale or media event. A rank never contributes points to its own evidence.

Convert continuous standing to the existing rank names using ordered bands. Fit band boundaries on a frozen reference or a lagged, slowly refreshed market reference, then freeze the chosen policy for paired runs. Use distinct entry and exit boundaries to prevent flicker. Evaluate the highest supported band from the current snapshot, allowing a strong breakout to skip a band and cooling to settle at any band supported by remaining draw. Distinguish low standing from missing measurement. Sparse or unavailable evidence uses an explicit fallback while genuine quiet weeks decay; zero response has a defined value. Avoid per-week rank quotas and consecutive chart-week counters. Record threshold crossings and near misses, with the evidence and reference used at the time.

For the first shadow implementation, calculate each route's support for each band from its normalized response, durable draw, and reach. Use the maximum of recent response and durable draw within a route when both measure the same audience. Weight a route's contribution by the reach appropriate to the band, using a continuous weight so wider distribution can compensate for less concentrated demand. Take the maximum support across eligible routes for that band. Starting at Superstar, evaluate each band in descending order. Use its entry boundary for bands above the prior standing and its lower exit boundary for the prior band and bands below it. Select the first supported band and otherwise report below Rising. Verify that support for higher bands is at least as demanding as support for lower ones. This policy has one stateful input, the previous band for hysteresis. The fitted references, reach weights, smoothing rates, and boundaries belong in a versioned calibration table.

Below Rising is an evidence result. Present it through the existing signing or decline context as appropriate, with the active contract and terminal status resolved independently. Keep a shadow snapshot in the diagnostic path during observation. Persist the dated snapshot and hysteresis state when the first causal reader needs continuity across saves.

An AI label can then act on observed draw through a bounded reader such as an album forecast. Extra investment can expose the act to more listeners, whose later purchases or following determine whether it advances again. This is the feedback loop to test. Keep a trace of the original exposure, the later response, and the decision that followed. In particular, audit whether follower-generated launch purchases are also increasing their own source following and later stature. Report how much promotion survives when evidence already attributable to the act's followers is discounted in shadow. Use separately attributable units where available and mark any proxy as such. The veteran screen sizes the whole follower feature. An independent stock-versus-intent screen needs fixed live draw and programme audience reads across the compared arms.

### Instrumentation and compatibility

1. Freeze the current assembly, source revision, profile, explicit flags and seeds; verify the baseline before editing. The combined integration flags are the intended starting point, but record the resolved flags and explicit overrides: defaults may have changed. Retain the existing cooling, recognition and programme settings. Run a same-build baseline and shadow with identical seeds, and retain complete logs and manifests.
2. Inventory each actual decision site and its timing before implementing a reader. `AILabel.GetMarketingBudget` selects a rung-based multiplier; promotion consumes the budget, while `ApplyPromotionSnapshot` and `ApplyReleasePromotion` use the rung for **stock only when recognition is disabled**. With recognition enabled they use cooled launch recognition for stock. Do not add a second career stock multiplier. Album prior uses `GetAlbumPriorCareerMultiplier`; career-banded format memory represents historical observations and is not a budget table. `ContractPricing` uses the greater of current and before-drop rungs; `RosterManager.ShouldResignArtist` unconditionally protects Star and above. Audit all call sites, including deferred album snapshots, projections, tranche spend and player-owned label paths, before defining scope.
3. Shadow-log at each candidate decision: date, act, label and tier, ownership, rung and before-drop rung, cohort (first observed chart year, explicitly left-censored at start), reader and decision context; raw chart-form window and units, following by age and region, format-specific advance intent, raw and cooled recognition, normalization reference and each component; proposed score, winning component, proposed band, current and counterfactual table values, eligibility, and reason for missing data. Capture pre-decision inputs only, including decisions that do not result in a release or contract. Record label capacity, available funds and LP commitments where relevant. Do not consume RNG or alter state, scheduling, saves, or economic results.
4. Log the handoff's trailing two-year peak-units proposal as a comparison input, with format, date, window, and units. The current career ladder itself counts chart milestones. For following, log national totals and reachable-region intent for singles and albums separately, with the configured per-follower rates and scale. A closed book's stocked copies measure fulfillment capacity. Log the version and effective date of each reference distribution, plus raw and effective recognition. The latter can already carry a Top 40-based cooling adjustment. Keep it diagnostic in the first candidate so it cannot reintroduce a rank clock through the back door.
5. Test candidate normalization offline on baseline shadow data. Compare a fixed reference with a trailing, lagged national top-N or percentile over eligible acts, with minimum population and denominator floors, clipping to [0,1], deterministic ties, and explicit cold-start and missing-data handling. Publish the exact candidate parameters and distribution by year, cohort, label tier and genre before choosing one. Prevent future leakage and self-normalization of the act being evaluated. Check whether scores drift as the simulated population and followings grow. Do not choose N or thresholds solely to force a target chart result.

## Phase 1: mapping and reader screens

Make the first treatment a single bounded read of shadow-proven standing, preferably the album forecast, so the progression signal can be tested with known exposure and spending constraints. Use distinct observe and causal switches. Writing the derived rank directly into `careerState` would change multiple readers simultaneously. Before any career display or shared rank cutover, inventory every writer and reader, including signing and comeback initialization, opening careers, senior cooling, chart callbacks, album milestones, contract evaluation, renewal, and roster departure. Treat the published weekly standing as the sole prestige writer for participating AI acts at cutover. The legacy hit callbacks still record chart history and contract evidence. Existing launch recognition cooling can continue independently in the pilot.

Specify initialization in terms of available evidence. Use existing seeded audiences and market evidence for opening acts, then flag any temporary fallback to their authored rank and fade it as observed evidence becomes available. A roster move or re-signing carries the act's measured audience forward, while contract probation resets according to contract rules. Use stored observation dates and the same initialization path for older saves missing the new snapshot. Keep terminal lifecycle states authoritative. Check weekly idempotence, no future reads, save/resume parity, and deterministic normalization and tie behavior.

Fit and report candidate score-to-table mappings against the baseline rung distribution and actual decisions, including cross-tabs and mismatch rates. Exact current money for *every* act cannot be guaranteed by a shared score when multiple rungs overlap at the same score. Define the intended invariant precisely: when the mapped band equals the current rung, the same existing table value must result. If the intended policy is uplift-only, state and test the rung floor explicitly; it makes the intervention asymmetric and can preserve incumbent advantages. Do not silently assume it follows from `max` over evidence. Declining and Dropped, before-drop contract treatment, missing evidence and cooling require explicit rules and fixtures before a causal run.

Screen readers independently behind separate opt-in switches. Compare album forecast and marketing budget as separate candidates. Marketing directly affects campaign exposure, so give it its own arm. Preserve existing cash, reserve, capacity and LP-plan constraints, and ensure forecasts and realized commitments use a documented, consistent decision-time snapshot. The format-memory band selects a reference population and needs its own design and test. Contract pricing and renewal protection are separate later screens. Preserve before-drop semantics in the control and measure how stale evidence affects renewal in any treatment. Keep existing programme scheduling in the reference configuration. Major bidding, poaching, and new player-facing reports require separate directives and evidence.

For each reader, log old and proposed values, actual applied value, floors/caps, snapshot timing and the affected decision. Add focused fixtures for component boundaries, zero/missing inputs, ties, normalization, rung-equivalent table values, cooled recognition, before-drop contracts, deferred projects and disabled-mode behavior. Verify save/load and deterministic behavior if stored state or snapshots are introduced. The disabled and shadow modes must leave existing decisions and RNG unchanged; compare all common economy outputs byte-for-byte against the same-build baseline on two seeds, with completion markers and error checks. Explain any output schema differences separately.

## Phase 2: causal evaluation and decision

Require trajectory evidence before judging the aggregate. Include one regional-led rise, one sustained record-led rise, one conversion-led rise if observed, and an adult LP path. Include near misses and cases with strong exposure followed by weak response, a declining incumbent, and a comeback. Trace weekly evidence, baseline and proposed rank, promotion or cooling cause, first qualifying response, release and media opportunities, spend, and subsequent observed demand. Report transitions without recent chart milestones, chart milestones without transitions, band dwell and churn, jumps, route shares, and outcomes for opening acts versus newly formed acts. A rare route absent from a seed should appear as a coverage gap, with the closest observed cases. Synthetic fixtures can prove the model permits each trajectory. Natural traces across seeds are the check that the simulation actually produces it.

Use a same-build paired baseline and one-reader treatment, same starting state, seed and resolved configuration. Run full decades for seeds 1001 and 2002 before adoption; preserve manifests, assembly hash, completion markers, errors and analysis scripts. Report annual results and both seed-level outcomes, not only their average. Simulated worlds diverge: paired deltas are net system effects, not direct effects on matched acts. Follow an initial screen with additional seeds if signs conflict or the decision is sensitive.

Report 1964 Top 10 and Hot 100 slot-weeks for the first-observed 1960 cohort and 1963–64 entrants; 1968 Top 10 share of the 1960–64 cohort; entrants' count and weeks per charting act, cohort survival and Top 10 entry. Use the historical series and matching caveats in `VeteranChartInvestigation.md`. Also report Top 40 repeat-hit share using a fixed two-year lookback, leading-act entry concentration, release and LP programme counts and timing, single and album market volume, budgets and realized campaign spend, contract offers and terms, Major share, label closures and artist/label tier distributions. Stratify by component driving stature, career rung and cohort. Include all eligible decisions in the denominator; successful-release-only comparisons are descriptive.

Compare historical benchmarks where available, and show tradeoffs rather than asserting a single kill threshold without an authorized target. An apparent improvement in cohort mix that destroys repeat hits, suppresses LPs, or shifts label survival needs further investigation. Document statistical and simulation limitations, missing outputs and seed disagreement. Do not adopt a reader, set numerical mapping, or implement acquisitions from this directive alone; return the measured alternatives and a recommendation for an explicit decision.
