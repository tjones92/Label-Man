# Label balance after the national-audience screen

24 October 2026. Read-only diagnosis and an isolated spacing experiment. The
working candidate remains `.6 + audience`; this investigation does not adopt new
defaults. Source context: [NationalFanbaseScreenResults.md](NationalFanbaseScreenResults.md).

The strongest current lead is **how labels allocate release opportunities to
known acts**, especially the Big Five's repeated use of the six-week single
floor. A broad studio cash subsidy or stronger returning-buyer multiplier is not
the first intervention supported by these observations. This is a diagnosis of
channels and an experiment, not proof that one parameter explains the entire
imbalance.

**User direction, 24 October:** differences between firms should emerge from
shared rules and their evolving circumstances. Use individual firms as diagnostic
cases, not separate calibration targets. Do not add named-label success bonuses,
release quotas, bespoke career templates or corrective rules keyed to a firm's
identity. Test common responses to repertoire, roster, audience, staffing,
finances, distribution and competing campaigns, then inspect the resulting
variation across firms and seeds.

## The discrepancy is real, with measurement limits

The four completed 106-week arms use seeds 1001, 2002 and 3003. Annual metrics
cover the complete 1960 and 1961 audit years; values are equal-weight means of
seed/year metrics. A repeat act has at least two distinct Top 10 records present
in that year, including carryovers. Its group is the selling label at its first
Top 10 that year, even if the act later moves. This differs from counting only
records issued that year or crediting every record to its original producer.

| Annual outcome | Recovered charts | Support off | .6 | Audience alone | Blend |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Big Five repeat acts | 6.0 | 8.33 | 8.0 | 9.67 | 11.0 |
| Studio repeat acts | 5.0 | 2.83 | 2.5 | 2.67 | 2.5 |
| Field repeat acts | 8.5 | 7.33 | 9.5 | 8.33 | 8.17 |
| Big Five Top 10 slot share | 28.8% | 33.0% | 30.4% | 32.7% | 34.7% |
| Studio Top 10 slot share | 19.0% | 12.6% | 12.2% | 12.5% | 12.1% |

The imbalance predates the audience reader. The blend exacerbates the Big Five
repeat count without filling the studio gap. It also has 22.33 annual Big Five
Top 10 acts against history's 18.5, and 10.0 studio acts against 12.5. Both the
breadth of successful acts and the fraction repeating differ; it is not solely
one prolific superstar on either side.

Historical chart entries identify credited sides, while the model represents
one side per single and uses persistent artist identities. Two explicit checks
do not remove the discrepancy:

- Anchored same-act, same-owner debut clusters at one or three weeks reduce
  historical studio repeaters from 5.0 to 4.5; Big Five repeaters stay 6.0.
  These are event proxies, not verified physical singles. The anchor prevents a
  succession of nearby entries from chaining into an arbitrarily long event.
- Merging just the Elvis/Jordanaires and Ray Charles/Raelettes backing-credit
  variants changes the side-based figures to 5.5 Big Five and 5.5 studio acts.
  Combining that limited alias sensitivity with the clustering gives 5.5 and
  5.0. Duets remain separate. This is not an exhaustive artist authority file.

January 1960 first observations are left censored. Neither sensitivity replaces
the published historical target. Their ranges, 5.5–6.0 Big Five and 4.5–5.5
studio repeaters, leave the direction of the imbalance intact.

## Release opportunity is more asymmetric than per-entry success

These are the original complete first-observed-hit cohorts: non-opening hits
from weeks 5–54, followed through exactly 52 further weeks. Silent and unowned
acts remain in denominators. Owner group is fixed at the original hit, so it
need not match the annual ownership assignment above.

| Blend outcome, mean of seed metrics | Big Five | Studios | Field |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Hit acts per seed | 19.67 | 10.67 | 40.0 |
| Singles released in next 52 weeks per act | 6.90 | 4.47 | 3.80 |
| Median single-to-single release gap, weeks | 6.0 | 8.17 | 9.33 |
| Follow-up release gaps below 13 weeks | 97.0% | 71.4% | 66.2% |
| Subsequent chart debuts per act | 4.73 | 2.94 | 2.01 |
| Eligible follow-ups entering by launch + 13 weeks | 69.2% | 63.7% | 55.0% |
| Top 10s per subsequent chart entry | 23.4% | 20.6% | 23.1% |

The Big Five offer about 54% more follow-up singles per hit act than studios.
Their advantage in Top 10 conversion per chart entry is much smaller. Relative
to `.6` alone, the blend raises entry from 58.8% to 69.2% for the Big Five and
56.9% to 63.7% for studios. Big Five follow-up release volume actually falls from
7.38 to 6.90, so the blend's additional three annual repeaters cannot simply be
described as three repeaters caused by more releases. Entry, world composition,
contracts and competition change together.

The existing historical fixed-window reader gives 3.39 later charted sides per
Big Five hit act and 3.78 per studio hit act, versus 4.73 and 2.94 in the blend.
However, clustering historical entries gives 2.72 and 2.67 event proxies. That
caveat matters especially for studios: a charted-side deficit is not a measured
deficit of physical release opportunities. Historical Top 10 conversion per
subsequent charted side is 24.6% Big Five and 20.6% studio, close to the model's
conditional rates. The small historical cohorts and literal credit names limit
precision.

## What is and is not blocking studio releases

The audit counts unique single intentions proposed by week 90, giving the
eight-week planning horizon and eight-week expiry room to resolve before week
106. Repeated weekly postponements count once. A cash-blocked intention that
later issues is retained as both temporarily blocked and issued. These are
single intentions, not every launch: an LP intention can also produce a promo
single. An intention never created is outside the denominator.

| Early planned single intentions, blend | Big Five | Studios | Field |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Eventually issued | 61.4% | 91.9% | 87.9% |
| Ever refused for unreserved cash | 4.9% | 2.3% | 10.2% |
| Expired | 35.1% | 5.6% | 6.6% |

The 91.9% is an execution rate conditional on a dated intention having been
created. It does not measure the share of all candidate artists, songs or
possible projects approved. Planning already restricts candidates by roster,
artist availability, release spacing and conflicting intentions; cash and
material are checked later, so these are not fully funded commitments either.

The high rate is partly a consequence of the experimental work-budget rule:
non-Major firms' dated plans are their work budget, with no second preparation
or issue-capacity lottery. This deliberately avoids losing the same unit of work
to repeated capacity draws. Cash, material, economics, promotion and expiry can
still stop it. Major firms retain a different execution path, so their 61.4%
is not an independent standard of desirable editorial selectivity.

**There is no established target for this completion percentage.** A selective
planner with reliable execution and an overpermissive planner can both issue
92% of their plans. The studio firms average 33.78 single launches per label
over 106 weeks, approximately 16.6 per year, including launches outside this
intention denominator. This supplies scale, not a historical output benchmark.
Neither the completion percentage nor the lower follow-up count alone proves
that studios should release more records.

The next shared-rule diagnosis should examine what receives a plan, the acts
and material passed over, forecast and realized returns, and cash/staff/campaign
load. It may identify excess weak projects, poor allocation to promising
follow-ups, or missing opportunities. Avoid imposing extra cancellations merely
to lower completion. Major firms' expired intentions and concentration of
successful acts near the six-week floor likewise call for inspection of the
common allocator, rather than more capacity to clear its queue.

Equal 13-week demand windows also weaken the broad cash/stock hypothesis:
studios service 92.7% of raw intent against 94.2% for the Big Five, and clear
46.6% of serviceable intent against 47.5%. Misses use the full window; only
verified post-retirement weeks receive zero demand. Median launch awareness is
.836 for studios and .814 for the Big Five. Studio stock and campaign inputs
are lower (about 5,834 vs 8,074 units; campaign impact .523 vs .788), but average
follow-up material quality is close (.639 vs .648). These inputs warrant
controlled tests, not an inference that every lower input caused a miss.

Returning buyers contribute 11.7% of studio follow-ups' first-13-week cleared
units, compared with 8.5% for the Big Five. The audience mechanism reaches studio
acts; its aggregate share is not unusually weak there. These percentages are
attribution within each world, not causal sales increments.

## Firm variation as evidence for shared mechanisms

| Firm | Historical annual Top 10 slots | Blend annual Top 10 slots | Blend annual repeat acts |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| MGM | 34.5 | 19.5 | .50 |
| ABC-Paramount | 18.5 | 10.0 | .33 |
| Warner Bros. | 15.5 | 19.5 | 1.00 |
| United Artists | 13.5 | 4.83 | .17 |
| Dot / Paramount | 9.5 | 9.17 | .50 |
| Colpix / Columbia Pictures-Screen Gems | 7.5 | 0.0 | 0.0 |

Slot credit follows the actual seller/owner in each chart week; repeat credit
follows the act's first Top 10 seller that year. Names have cosmetic suffixes
across seeds, so model aggregation uses stable label IDs. Warner is already
above this short-window historical slot benchmark and Dot is close. These
comparisons diagnose the aggregate discrepancy; they are not desired outcomes
to enforce for the individual labels.

Colpix is the exception to the broad finance finding: 3.33 of its 12.0 early
single intentions per seed encounter cash refusal, versus .67 for MGM, .33 for
ABC and none for Warner, UA or Dot. These cases distinguish circumstances the
same rules encounter. Trace cash-constrained plans wherever they occur and
compare them with similarly situated firms; test the same decision rule on all
eligible firms. MGM, ABC and UA account for much of the remaining slot deficit,
providing cases to inspect repertoire and work allocation without assigning
those names a compensating parameter or a prescribed trajectory.

## Code paths implicated

- `LabelReleaseProgramme.MonthlyCapacity` gives Majors ten single slots per month
  under the experiment. Work-budget MidTiers take `releasesPerMonth` times the
  MidTier multiplier, currently one; the template rate starts around 1–2.5.
  Staffing and seasonal factors still apply.
- `CompetitorManager.PlanProgramme` gives overdue recent Top 40 acts a priority
  boost at 13 weeks, but career priority can select them earlier whenever the
  six-week `SingleFloor` permits. The overdue threshold is not a 13-week release
  schedule. Multiple spaced intentions for the same act are allowed.
- `OpeningCareerService` reserves eight studio opening careers from the existing
  global career budget, choosing acts within each studio's roster/genre pool.
  Those careers are not missing. Their material and opportunities can still
  differ from the globally selected remaining canopy.
- `StudioParentLabels.SupportMonth` funds existing commitments and acquisitions,
  explicitly excluding soft programme holds. Parent capital is not an additional
  release-slot budget. Its behavior should not be inferred from cash alone.

## Controlled spacing pilot

The pilot retains the complete blend bundle and changes only
`--slate-single-floor=13`, for every AI label. It runs a fresh six-week control
and a 13-week treatment with the same assembly and seed 1001 for 106 weeks.
This is a broad scheduling ablation: it affects cold acts and studios as well
as Major hit acts. It cannot isolate a Major-only mechanism or establish an
optimal release interval.

Both arms completed, with the same assembly, all native completion checks and
authored-purse reconciliation. The fresh control matches the archived blend in
66 checked week-keyed economic/chart streams through week 105, before the
terminal snapshot boundary. The following figures are **seed 1001 only**; annual
rows average its two complete years. They must not replace the three-seed means
above.

| Seed-1001 outcome | Six-week control | 13-week floor |
|---|---:|---:|
| Annual Big Five repeat acts | 12.0 | 7.0 |
| Annual studio repeat acts | 2.0 | 2.0 |
| Annual field repeat acts | 7.0 | 10.0 |
| Big Five Top 10 slot share | 34.9% | 26.3% |
| Studio Top 10 slot share | 10.3% | 10.5% |
| Field Top 10 slot share | 54.8% | 63.3% |
| Single launches over 106 weeks | 7,134 | 5,943 |
| Cleared single units, millions | 294.52 | 286.25 |
| Serviceable single intent cleared | 49.9% | 58.0% |
| Field later chart debuts per hit act, 52 weeks | 1.69 | 1.56 |
| Field eligible follow-up entry rate | 46.9% | 66.4% |
| Opening field MidTier closures | 13 | 13 |

The intervention achieves its timing change: median Big Five release spacing
becomes 13 weeks, and no captured follow-up gaps are below 13 weeks in any
owner group. Big Five follow-ups fall from 6.95 to 3.60 per hit act. Studio
follow-ups also fall, from 4.0 to 2.6. Their conditional Top 10 conversion rises,
but their annual repeater count and slot share do not meaningfully improve.

This is evidence that release spacing can substantially move Big Five repeat
success in this world, while **reducing Big Five success is not sufficient to
restore studio success**. The freed chart share mainly goes to the field.
Higher entry probability accompanies fewer opportunities and fewer subsequent
chart debuts per field hit act; selecting the intervention on the repeat total
alone would miss that career cost. Cohorts differ between the worlds, so these
are system outcomes, not effects on matched individual artists.

**Disposition:** retain the 13-week setting as an unselected diagnostic. Do not
adopt a universal cap or a uniform studio bonus from this pilot. The next
targeted tests should distinguish the common allocator's repeated early
selection of known acts from repertoire selection, resource constraints and
missing opportunities. Trace the same decisions across all eligible firms,
including the cash-constrained cases observed at Colpix. Let different firm
outcomes emerge from their state and shared decision rules. Keep the
earned-audience candidate and evaluate continuing careers alongside owner
shares. Replication and a narrower scheduling intervention are needed before a
policy selection.

## Reproduction and verification

The read-only ledger is generated by:

```powershell
python SimTools/analyze_label_balance.py --review SimLogs/afb-v2/review `
  --arms blend f06 --output SimLogs/label-balance-v1
```

It writes `opportunities.csv`, `labels.csv`, equal-window `demand.csv`,
`historical-sensitivity.csv`, `historical-acts.csv`, `observations.md` and the
source manifest. Generated evidence stays under gitignored `SimLogs/`.

The completed pilot is under `SimLogs/label-balance-v1-pilot-local/`; its review
contains the separate `pilot-review/` annual, career, release and economic
ledgers. The control replay proof is
`SimLogs/label-balance-v1/control-identity.json`.

The spacing experiment is reproducible with `RunLabelBalanceAudit.ps1`; supply
an unused `-RunTag`. Analyze its completed plan with:

```powershell
python SimTools/analyze_label_balance.py --pilot-plan SimLogs/TAG/plan.json `
  --output SimLogs/TAG/review
```

The pilot reader rejects different assemblies, extra policy changes, incomplete
seeds, duplicate seeds and changes to the authored consumer purse. Analysis
fixtures cover ownership movers, anchored side clustering, deduplicated slot
refusals, equal demand windows, missing observations, retired misses and pilot
configuration matching.

Final verification passed: clean build; 19 Python regression tests; 1,023 native
fit-support assertions, 231 audience/renewal assertions and 47 stocking
assertions; full-world JSON and actual gzip save/load followed by resumed play
with live audience stocks and title depletion. Both pilot worlds also pass the
audience stock, attribution and depletion readers. Logs and hashes are under
`SimLogs/label-balance-v1/`. No game defaults were changed, and no simulation
processes remain running after verification.
