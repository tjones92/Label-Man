# Professional songs versus covers under the full profile

Follow-up: `WritingSupplyAllocationAndFlagAudit.md` tests rock-writer supply,
joins album material to actual single plans, and audits inactive integration
settings. `WritingCombinedIntegrationScreen.md` records the subsequently
requested broad two-seed combined-feature screen and its compatibility blockers.

2026-11-02–03. This investigation follows `WritingFanbaseComparison.md` using the
ordinary-game profile, causal media/fanbase bundle and combined writing workflow.
It tests the existing accessible-sampling intervention on fresh worlds. Gameplay
defaults, writer abilities, fees, rights, capacity and commissioning rules are
unchanged.

## Target and definitions

The historical **1–2 covers per fresh professional song** sensitivity range in
`FreshMaterialHistoricalCalibration.md` is a working calibration assumption, not
a measured historical distribution. Its central 4:3 ratio is also a hypothesis.
The [Library of Congress's account of Goffin and King's Aldon work for the Shirelles](https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/Tapestry.pdf)
supports the professional-introduction mechanism, not these percentages.

There is some quantitative authorship context: [Shourin Sen's 2007 article](https://jolt.law.harvard.edu/articles/pdf/v21/21HarvJLTech233.pdf)
reports that 22% of Billboard's 1960 year-end top fifty were written by their
performers (Figure 1; underlying data described as on file with the author).
That chart-selected authorship statistic does not distinguish fresh outside
introductions from covers and cannot validate a release-wide covers/professional
target. A future historical sample needs both writer credits and prior commercial
recording dates, with explicit rules for collaborations and simultaneous versions.

Here professional means the simulation's `ExternalProfessional` launch source.
Covers combine `CoverStandard` and `CoverRecentHit`; traditional/public-domain
material remains separate. A cover can itself have a professional songwriter.
The question is the recording's material route, not the author's occupation.

Two denominators are kept separate:

- Mature intentions: singles proposed in weeks 1–78 and issued within 26 weeks
  of proposal, counted once per intention using actual launch provenance.
- Complete conversion-window launches: singles issued in weeks 1–91, using the
  existing concentration analyzer's through-issue-week-plus-13 convention.
  These reproduce the original comparison's professional-share denominator.

The three full-profile controls have 1,001 professional issues and 10,288 covers
among 17,325 issued mature intentions: **5.8% professional and 10.28 covers per
professional song**. Reaching 2:1 at fixed issue volume, entirely through
cover-to-professional substitution, would require about 2,762 substitutions and
3,763 professional issues, approximately 3.8 times the current count. This is
arithmetic about the size of the gap, not a predicted intervention effect or quota.

## Result: discovery roughly doubles professional use, but leaves a large gap

All three matched access treatments completed 104 weeks successfully. The
direction is consistent across seeds. Mature issued intentions give:

| Seed | Issued intentions, control → access | Professional issues | Covers | Covers per professional |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1001 | 5,823 → 5,754 | 310 → 617 | 3,409 → 3,172 | 11.00 → 5.14 |
| 2002 | 5,787 → 5,825 | 344 → 687 | 3,505 → 3,346 | 10.19 → 4.87 |
| 3003 | 5,715 → 5,711 | 347 → 644 | 3,374 → 3,245 | 9.72 → 5.04 |
| Pooled | 17,325 → 17,290 | 1,001 → 1,948 | 10,288 → 9,763 | **10.28 → 5.01** |

Professional share rises **5.8% → 11.3%** in this intention cohort. The original
comparison's complete conversion-window launch denominator independently gives
**5.76% → 11.32%**, with 1,065 → 2,092 professional launches among
18,474 → 18,473 singles. Its covers/professional ratio moves 10.38 → 5.02.
Thus the original 5.3–6.0% seed range becomes **10.9–11.8%** under the access
intervention. Discovery matters in these modeled worlds, but this policy alone
does not reach the working 1–2:1 range.

The 947 additional professional issues are not 947 observed cover substitutions.
Cover issues fall by 525, artist-written issues by 340 and traditional issues by
117; total issue count also changes. World trajectories and issued intentions
can differ. No individual substitution or historical effect is identified.

The first-review observer shows the intended route changing: professional
selections rise from 698 to 3,031 and starts from 247 to 1,280. Reviews with
neither an accessible sampled professional song nor owned delivery fall from
17,401/19,587 (88.8%) to 12,953/19,563 (66.2%). These are reviews and sampled
opportunities, not unique songs or the full set of material routes. Later start
failures remain substantial: label-capacity blocks rise 311 → 800 and the grouped
other/artist-license blocks 103 → 579. Treatment sampling still tests label-level
access; an act-specific discovery variant is a separate untested hypothesis.

Fees include commissions, licenses and retainers across all 104 weeks, not only
the mature intention cohort. They rise in every seed, by $92,401 pooled (8.1%).
Endpoint unused licenses rise 66 → 116; this is a snapshot, not proof of permanent
waste. The middle cohort's count and proportion improve in all three seeds, but
hit-act counts fall in two and fixed top-30 concentration rises in two. Cohorts
and retrospective leaders may change identity; these are aggregate world
outcomes, not paired individual effects. Label failures are mixed. This is useful
ratio evidence with unresolved economic and breadth tradeoffs; keep accessible
sampling experimental rather than adopting it as a general balance fix.

The residual imbalance is particularly large in RockAndRoll: access changes its
pooled mature ratio from 48.55 to 24.40, still only 57 professional issues versus
1,391 covers. TraditionalPop moves 23.99 → 11.31, Country 9.15 → 5.16, DooWop
5.74 → 3.55, EasyListening 7.86 → 2.79 and Soul 5.09 → 2.00. Genre mix therefore
cannot be repaired by assuming that the aggregate discovery gain solves each
genre's access or supply problem.

## Independent stock diagnosis

The controls contain another plausible allocation constraint. Excluding demos
already marked recorded in the opening snapshot, there are 4,693 newly recorded
professional demos across the three worlds. Of those, 3,202 (68.2%) have an
observed released professional album use but no observed professional single use
by the endpoint. Another 708 appear in both, 401 only in singles, and 382 have no
observed released use. These are unique demo/first-client destinations, not
release counts, and do not require mature follow-up windows. An album use does
not establish that a song would have been suitable, profitable or a hit as a single.

At week 104 the controls have 370, 380 and 371 demos marked `Available`. This
world inventory is not synonymous with accessible, affordable, genre-compatible
material for a particular act. Nevertheless, recording predominantly for albums
while leaving other stock available makes allocation and discovery useful
hypotheses alongside total writer capacity.

`ProfessionalWritingMarket.MarkRecorded` removes recorded demos from ordinary
professional first-recording discovery; `CanLicense` preserves the original
client's control. `AiRepertoireService.PickSong` already filters professional
arrivals through act-level visibility. The experimental switch tested here
affects the separate programme-search route, whose four offers otherwise come
from the raw primary-genre pool before access checks.

## Matched experiment

Each treatment adds only `--material-accessible-sampling` to its corresponding
104-week full-profile control, for seeds 1001, 2002 and 3003. It filters the
professional pool through the existing **label-level** access predicate before
the same four deterministic draws. Artist-specific licensing is still checked
when work starts. Neither rights nor affordability is relaxed, and the final
choice may still be a cover or original. The changed sample also changes the
programme's session-expectation baseline, so this tests the complete discovery
policy rather than an isolated extra offer.

Controls are `writing-full-profile-market-1001` and
`writing-full-profile-extension-market-{2002,3003}`. Treatments use
`writing-full-access-market-{1001,2002,3003}`. All use frozen assembly
`511E9709FE20879CE4636C7FFE39F9C62E9CE825DC8EB01A110041711D221AC5`.

## Genre-supply omission identified by inspection

The ordinary profile does **not** enable `--rock-hit-writers`. Without it,
`GenerateProfessionalPool` has no publishing office focused on RockAndRoll or
DooWop. R&R also inherits the rock-family 1960 professional source weight of
.05; the existing switch supplies a genre-specific .35 anchor and an additional
office focused on RockAndRoll, DooWop and RnB. Commissioned work can still create
genre-specific material without that office, so this is not a claim that all
professional routes are absent.

Among the controls' mature issued intentions, RockAndRoll has only 29 professional
issues versus 1,408 covers across 2,939 issues (48.55:1). TraditionalPop is also
imbalanced at 1,871:78 (23.99:1), Country at 1,162:127 (9.15:1), and Soul at
417:82 (5.09:1). These are launch-genre populations, not matched cohorts.

This makes the existing rock-writer switch a concrete next supply hypothesis.
It is a bundle: it changes office creation, staff distribution, source weights
and availability, not merely access. Its earlier calibration results cannot be
assumed to carry over to the finite market and full profile. A separate matched
test should precede adoption; it should not be bundled into the discovery
experiment above or represented as a tested result of this investigation.

The runner now accepts `-RockHitWriters`, recording the existing engine switch
in the manifest. A one-week full-profile smoke run for seed 1001 completed
successfully with that option on the same assembly and no reported engine
errors. This checks the option's execution, not its effect on the ratio. A future
104-week supply arm can use the reproduction command below with a fresh
`-Tag writing-full-rock-supply`, replacing `-AccessibleProfessionalSampling`
with `-RockHitWriters`; retain the full-profile controls for a separate comparison.

## Next controlled changes

1. Test the existing rock-writer supply bundle independently under the full
   finite-market profile, with the same three seeds and complete windows. Track
   RockAndRoll and DooWop separately as well as the aggregate. This directly
   addresses the missing office and low professional opportunity weight.
2. Investigate single/album allocation with actual act/song/plan joins. Determine
   whether an act with an upcoming single records a strong professional song only
   as an album track, what song the single then uses, whether an existing master
   could legitimately serve that single, and the perceived quality/cost tradeoff
   at the time. The endpoint album-only count is a lead for this test, not a reason
   to reserve all good songs for singles or to grant extra recording rights.

Quantity-only commission triggers and plan timing remain plausible demand
constraints, but the earlier reminder pilot had mixed professional-use and career outcomes and
cash-reserve refusals were a small ordered category in the controls. These data
do not select a cash subsidy, lower writer fees or immediate buying without a
production plan as the next intervention. Additional seeds and a later-era holdout
are needed before changing ordinary gameplay policy.

## Reproduction

Run each seed with a fresh tag; the runner does not refuse existing output names.

```powershell
./SimTools/RunWritingMarketCalibration.ps1 -Arm market -Workflow combined -Seeds 1001,2002,3003 -Weeks 104 -Tag writing-full-access -CausalFanbase -DefaultProfile -ConcentrationAudit -MaterialFunnelAudit -AccessibleProfessionalSampling -ExpectedAssemblySha256 511E9709FE20879CE4636C7FFE39F9C62E9CE825DC8EB01A110041711D221AC5
python SimTools/analyze_writing_full_access.py
python -m unittest discover -s SimTools -p test_writing_full_access.py
```

`analyze_writing_full_access.py` checks paired flags and seeds, common assembly,
104-week endpoints, completion markers, engine error logs, commission ledgers,
material provenance and no linked preparation before delivery. It emits pooled
and seed-level ratios, career and genre mixes, first-review funnels, stock
destinations, spending, unused licenses, label failures and career/concentration
measures in `SimLogs/writing-calibration/writing-full-access-comparison.json`.

The six new fixtures check treatment isolation, ratio definitions, opening-stock
exclusion, destination deduplication, conversion-window boundaries and pooled
counts. All 13 existing market, concentration and funnel fixtures also passed
(19 total). The three full treatments and rock-supply smoke completed without
reported engine errors; their runners exited successfully. Godot used approved
outside-sandbox execution after the sandboxed engine crashed.
No engine code changed, and the frozen assembly hash was verified again at the end.
