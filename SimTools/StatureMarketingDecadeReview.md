# Stature marketing decade review — 2026-11-06

## Recommendation

Keep the stature marketing reader experimental. These two completed paired decades do not establish a consistent improvement in cohort turnover or album volume. The reader raises marketing costs in both seeds, remains strongly biased toward upward budget changes, and produces opposing cohort results. Do not adopt this calibration by default from this screen.

There is no evidence here of a general collapse in LP output or label population. There is also no stable benefit that offsets the extra spending across both seeds. Two seeds are a sensitivity screen, not a statistical estimate of the treatment's average effect.

## Evidence and scope

All four runs, control and treatment for seeds 1001 and 2002, reached their exact 522-week completion markers. Both treatment workers exited zero with empty error logs, archived and verified 148 CSVs each, and completed the paired summaries against the retained 147-CSV controls. The extra treatment CSV is the nondeterministic performance profile. The completed one-year disabled/shadow neutrality evidence remains separate; decade-duration neutrality was not rerun.

The existing summary validates the paired assembly, engine, calibration, C# content hashes, revision and resolved economic flags, output manifests, budget-table arithmetic, unavailable fallback and snapshot timing. The marketing reader was the only causal stature reader. Controls used workstation GC; restarted treatments used four-heap server GC and performance profiling. Those are execution/observation differences, not intended economic interventions; this run does not add a same-arm decade GC equivalence experiment.

## Chart cohorts and repeat hits

Cohorts use each act's first observed Hot 100 year, including the opening chart. They are not true career debut cohorts: pre-1960 history is left-censored. Each 1964 and 1968 comparison has exactly 52 observed chart weeks, 5,200 Hot 100 slots and 520 Top 10 slots in all four runs. The historical 1964 old-cohort benchmark is 42/520 = **8.1%**. All four simulated worlds remain far above it. The historical 1968 early-cohort benchmark is 144/520 = **27.7%**. Seed 2002 moves toward it, while seed 1001 barely changes and remains above 64%. This is seed-sensitive redistribution, not a reliable resolution of the incumbent excess.

The pooled 1965–69 comparison uses 2,610 Top 10 slot-weeks per arm. Seed 2002 gives the cohort 83 fewer slots in 1968 and 81 more across the other four years. Seed 1001 gives the cohort 42 more slots over the five years. The full reader therefore shows little progress on the pooled late-decade share, even in the seed with the large 1968 improvement.

More 1963–64 entrants chart in 1964 in both treatments, but fewer 1967–68 entrants chart in 1968 in both. In seed 2002, the smaller late entrant group wins more Top 10 representation; seed 1001 loses such representation. Entrant count alone would miss this distinction.

A repeat Top 40 entry is a distinct record's first observed Top 40 week with another record by the same act entering strictly earlier in the preceding **104 weeks**. Simultaneous first entries do not make one another repeats. Opening-chart entry histories are left-censored; the 1964 and 1968 windows are fully inside the observed run.

|---|---:|---:|
| Profiled simulation time | **100.19 min** | **95.06 min** |

| Competitor weekly work, whole run | 51.5% of profiled time | 49.9% |

Both completed, archived and summarized in a batch lasting about **105.8 minutes** from restart to final summary. This is not a restored one-hour concurrent decade. The two-year probe was a reasonable configuration check but did not predict late-world cost. The final year takes roughly eleven times as long as the first. Late GC pauses increase substantially, but GC alone does not explain the growth; competitor weekly work is the largest measured block. Its spans include their children and GC pauses, so these shares must not be added as disjoint categories.

The earlier live CPU trace identified professional-writing-market and release-programme work as major costs. Current `ProfessionalWritingMarket.CommissionPlan` still filters the full release slate, despite the available per-label/artist indexing described in `BranchEndDecadeReview.md`; it is a concrete candidate to measure and optimize next. This analysis makes no additional code optimization or speedup claim. Controls and treatments have different economic trajectories and instrumentation, so their wall-time difference is not a controlled GC benchmark.

## Remaining coverage and next decision

This review establishes paired aggregate spending, annual volume, observed chart cohorts, fixed-lookback repeat hits, chart concentration, end-year populations and runtime. It does not complete the directive's route-by-route natural trajectory dossier, band dwell/churn analysis, matched opening-versus-new-formation survival, full artist contract-offer terms, or a follower-feedback ablation. Existing shadow traces could support further work; no follower sensitivity arm was run in this decade batch. Regional, adult-LP and conversion routes must not be declared validated from aggregate volume alone.

The next economic step should be a prespecified calibration/trajectory review, followed by additional paired seeds if this mapping remains a candidate. The direction disagreement in old-cohort share and LP volume is already sufficient to withhold default adoption. Keep the programme album reader, career display, contract readers and acquisition policies outside this decision. A separate performance-only follow-up can target writing-market/slate scans and prove output equivalence.
