# Phase 6 city-share review — October 9, 2026

Decision: retain the current version-2 placement weights. The old region-first distortion has not returned. Fresh two-seed censuses reproduce the previously accepted opening allocations; the 4,000-act reserve is checked separately. No established artist is redistributed.

This is a plausibility and implementation review of a selected commercial simulation cohort across 31 playable catchments. It is not a census of every performing act in America, a chart-share estimate, or a claim that each real city supported these percentages. Exact small-city and specialty-center weights remain authored priors.

## Evidence and boundaries

- [US Census Bureau, P25-427, Table 4, printed pp. 71–72](https://www2.census.gov/library/publications/1969/demographics/P25-427.pdf): April 1960 population on the report’s later SMSA boundaries provides a scale check. New York 10,692,633; Chicago 6,220,913; Los Angeles–Long Beach 6,038,771. The broad-center hierarchy is supported, but these are residents, not acts. The report’s later boundaries must not be silently labeled original 1960 boundaries. New England reporting also has different definitions.
- [Museum of the City of New York, Folk City](https://www.mcny.org/exhibition/folk-city): the Village’s clubs and coffeehouses support New York’s opportunity role alongside its broader institutions.
- [Stax Museum history](https://staxmuseum.org/stax-museums-electronic-press-kit/): Satellite began in 1957 and became Stax in 1961, supporting a specialized Memphis role before the mid-decade national soul boom. This cannot justify a literal percentage of all American acts.
- [HistoryMiami, Teen Miami](https://temp.historymiami.org/exhibition/teen-miami/): garage and R&B activity demonstrates that the smaller Miami weight does not mean an empty or single-genre scene.
- The repository historical atlas supports differentiated opportunities and continuity; it explicitly marks weaker small-city research. Famous recording destinations are not automatically everyone’s hometown.

## Findings

New York leads both seeds. Los Angeles and Chicago form the next broad tier; their small opening difference on seed 1001 is consistent with preserved genre mix and sampling. Detroit, Nashville and Memphis are specialized centers, with Nashville strengthened for country and Memphis/Detroit for soul/R&B. San Francisco retains early folk/jazz opportunities and gains additional psychedelic fit only later; no city’s general musical life is switched off.

All 31 places retain positive opportunity. Billings has 1/0 opening acts and 7/3 reserve acts, instead of the former roughly 5% national share. A zero in a finite launch draw is not a hard exclusion. The reserve gives a clearer picture of available scene residents than signed rosters alone. Do not add quotas or births to disguise local-label shortages.

The concentration is plausible for the selected recording-market cohort: approximately 40% is based in New York, Los Angeles and Chicago together. Specialty-center overrepresentation relative to residential population is intentional opportunity modeling, but remains the main historical uncertainty. No source reviewed measures a statistically representative 1960s act-population share, so this pass does not certify exact weights as historical measurements.

No population, genre, quality, RNG, headcount, formation budget or existing geography changes are justified by this pass. The existing formation-cohort/order/era checks also pass at both seeds. Longer-term city concentration and migration remain part of deferred decade acceptance.
