# Professional office songs versus covers

The larger goal is to make fresh professional office songs a more viable source for singles. We count them separately from covers and track both their absolute release count and covers per professional song among mature issued singles. The working range of one to two covers per professional song is a calibration hypothesis. [Original investigation](WritingFullProfileAccessInvestigation.md)

The two-seed combined profile raised professional issues from 654 to 1,350 and lowered the cover ratio from 10.57 to 4.10. Rock-writer supply alone helped Rock-and-Roll much more than it helped the overall mix. Album allocation remains relevant. In the combined runs, about 60% of newly recorded professional demos appeared only on released LPs. That share describes where songs went. Suitability for a single still needs to be assessed at the decision point. [Combined screen](WritingCombinedIntegrationScreen.md) · [Supply and allocation audit](WritingSupplyAllocationAndFlagAudit.md)

**The current question is whether smaller labels lose viable LP projects at the profitability gate or lose buyers after release.** The combined profile produced 70 net additional closures, including 22 solvent dormancies. Opening labels that closed only in the combined runs typically lost LP sales and record receipts before closing. Omitting LP assembly increased album releases from 8,109 to 8,788, while closures rose from 232 to 242. Professional use stayed near 12.5%. This makes the LP profitability forecast a more focused next test than another assembly comparison. [Turnover investigation](CombinedLabelTurnoverInvestigation.md) · [LP assembly experiment](LpAssemblyTurnoverExperiment.md)

For the next matched test, keep assembly, stock-aware costing, and the writing, media, and retail settings fixed. Isolate the LP profitability forecast and follow smaller labels from proposed project to approval, release, sales, and cash receipts. Track the absolute number of professional singles and the cover ratio alongside professional demo use on LPs. This will show whether changing the forecast improves release opportunities and whether those opportunities alter how professional material gets used. The prepared-single path still bypasses consideration of an eligible existing LP master, which remains a separate allocation experiment.
