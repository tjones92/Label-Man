# Entry concentration, chart breadth and repeat careers

2026-11-02. Final investigation of all four completed fresh-profile pairs, seeds 1001/2002/3003/4004.
All eight worlds completed. All figures use the complete `lp-retail-v2` profile plus the accepted causal media/follower settings.
The comparison is 1961–63; these are not updates to the old stripped-profile 1966 result.

The two concentration measures expose different things. The new Top 40 entries are concentrated among a small
group of serial hitmakers. Much of the Top 100 slot-week percentage gap comes from a broader, more transient
lower-chart population, which also makes its top-decile group larger. The middle of the repeat-career distribution
is thin in both arms.

The entry-share excess survives a fixed-headcount comparison. Overall new-hit volume and the number of hit acts are
close to history, but the top 20 take too many of those successes. The slot-week percentage has a different
denominator: it ranks every act appearing on the Top 100. The model's larger tail expands that group from 81 to about
106–107 actors. A fixed top-81 comparison is already close to history. This is an additional diagnostic, not a
replacement or redefinition of the original top-decile metric.

## The chart is turning over faster, not holding each leader's title longer

Observed Top 100 weeks per title are 7.37 in history, versus 6.40/6.35 in control/treatment. Within the top-decile
slot groups they are 8.17 historically, versus 6.93/6.79. These are observed durations within the same three-year
window, including boundary censoring; they are not completed lifetime estimates.

The slot-share decomposition is exact:

```
group's slot-week share = group's share of observed chart titles
                       × group's mean observed title duration / market mean duration
```

The model's leading decile supplies a larger fraction of the chart's distinct titles. Its titles do not have longer
observed runs than the historical leading decile. Faster blanket decay would move the already short runs further
from history. The excess lower-chart actor population needs its own explanation.

## Fast, highly successful leading acts; a thin repeat-career middle

In these completed worlds, retrospectively selected entry leaders receive about 6.7–7.0 singles per act-year in the
eligible release window, with a six-week median observed release gap. Other releasing acts average about 0.83–0.85
singles per act-year and a 15–17-week median among observed repeat releases. Career lengths and label memberships
differ, so those are descriptive rates, not exposure-adjusted causal estimates. Complete historical release
catalogues are unavailable here; no historical release-rate comparison is inferred from chart records alone.

The contrast remains within Major releases: entry leaders have six-week median gaps, versus 9–11 weeks for the
other releasing acts. Leaders' releases convert to the Top 40 within 13 weeks at 68–81% overall, versus about 5%
for others; almost all leaders' singles reach the Top 100. Leaders are selected retrospectively, so high conversion
is partly inherent in that selection and does not by itself identify the cause.

| New Top 40 entries per act within the three-year window | History | Control | Candidate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| One | 62.7% | 70.4% | 70.0% |
| Two to four | 26.5% | 21.9% | 22.7% |
| Five or more | 10.8% | 7.7% | 7.3% |

This describes entries within the window, not lifetime one-hit careers. A near-historical aggregate repeat share
can coexist with too many single-entry acts and a few extreme serial hitmakers. The candidate does not yet repair
that distribution.

A separate, fully observed model funnel follows each act's first observed new Top 40 title when it was launched as
NewSigning/Rising, restricting first hits to weeks 5 through end-minus-65. It then observes 52 weeks of releases plus
13 more weeks for their chart outcomes. Across the completed arms, these acts average 2.94–3.22 further singles in
the next year, with a 6–8-week median wait for the next release. About 8–13% release nothing further in that year;
72–78% have no further new Top 40 title, and 10–13% of the follow-up releases reach the Top 40 within 13 weeks.

This is not a historical first-ever-hit comparison: history lacks frozen launch-career states, and the observation
does not establish uninterrupted act availability. It does show why an interpretation based solely on absent or late
records is incomplete. Most of this cohort gets records into the market promptly; conversion and continuity of
support need tracing too. The funnel includes all single launch routes and retains acts with no later release.

## What the allocator and paired actors show

87–94% of selected entry-leader single intentions are planned less than 13 weeks after the previous single or earlier
open intention. The six-week feasibility floor permits these ordinary slots; the quarterly reference supplies
urgency later. Leader choices average career priority about .9 and momentum about .85, versus roughly .45 and .03
for others. Those are correlated observed inputs, not causal estimates of each coefficient's effect.

The new overdue bonus is almost zero for entry leaders (mean 0–.019 across these runs), because they are already
being supplied. The main concentration is present in the control. Existing career/momentum ranking, campaign
resources, the configured .6 recent-hit commercial-fit floor and returning-audience readers are candidates for
further attribution; the current data do not isolate their individual effects.

Rank membership also changes. In seed 1001 the control's same 22 entry-leading acts fall from 34.4% to 26.2% of
new-hit entries under treatment; in seed 2002 the same 20 fall from 38.2% to 33.2%. The treatment selects different
winners. A rising dynamic top-decile slot share therefore should not be read as the same incumbent actors gaining
longer runs. The frozen entry groups match both actor ID and reported name across the two worlds. Slot groups
contain later-created actors whose identities can diverge and are reported only for the matched subset.

## Next investigation

No further scheduling trials are warranted now; leave the completed candidate off by default. First trace the next record's entry conversion after a new hit, with attention to
campaign resources, continuing market access, returning buyers and the existing recent-hit protection. The observed
first-hit cohort already receives records fairly quickly; these observations do not isolate which reader or market
constraint prevents further chart success.

For allocation, ask who receives ordinary six-to-twelve-week releases while another viable act waits, separating
selection, material and entry conversion. The single contribution
prior used by the allocator has no explicit predecessor input, while actual launch later splits same-act radio and
cools predecessors. Testing an incremental timing read for a still-selling predecessor is a specific business
hypothesis; it is not yet an established defect or a request for an arbitrary universal cooldown.

Track the repeat-career middle and lower-chart-only population alongside both concentration measures and fixed-N
checks. Keep LP commitments and the demand calibration fixed. A later isolated reader sensitivity could distinguish
allocation from commercial-fit protection/returning demand; a blanket demand or decay change would conflate them.

## Evidence and method

`analyze_programme_concentration.py` reads only completed chart histories and does not touch simulations. It retains
the existing entry definition: first observed Top 40 entry per title, excluding opening titles. The historical source
uses as-printed artist/title credits; model actors use persistent IDs. Top-5% entry and top-10% slot groups are
different ranked sets drawn from different actor universes. Release conversions exclude the final 13 observation
weeks, and durations use equal calendar windows.

Generated evidence is under `SimLogs/followup-v4-fresh/concentration/`: `summary.csv`, `actors.csv`,
`release-groups.csv`, `major-release-groups.csv`, `choice-groups.csv`, `frozen-groups.csv` and `method.json`.
`first-observed-hit-funnel.csv` records the closed-follow-up cohort, with origin label tier frozen at launch when
available, and record-owner tier at the first hit reported separately.
Allocator code: `CompetitorManager.ReleaseProgramme.cs` (`PlanProgramme`, `CanProgrammeArtist`);
prior: `CompetitorManager.cs` (`BuildDecisionContext`, `SinglePriorNetAtBookedPrice`);
market response: `HitActFitSupport.cs`, `ArtistFanbase.cs`, and the launch's `HitActCadence` predecessor handling.
