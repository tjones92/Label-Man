### The economic hump is lifecycle and catalog composition

The accepted disabled control is a compatibility boundary with known legacy Dropped release behavior. In 1962:

- control has 2,886 Dropped decisions out of 3,615 total;
- treatment has 2,976 NewSigning decisions out of 3,529 total;
- control Dropped decisions average approximately 11.9K realized units in the decision ledger; and
- treatment NewSigning decisions average approximately 15.4K.

This is a direct consequence of the lifecycle model replacing release-eligible frozen Dropped output with ordinary active contracts. It is not a finance-ledger difference: units, gross, label net, and market net move together, with gross per unit nearly unchanged.

The 1962 current-release decision ledger is only about `1.015x` control in realized units, materially below the `1.1295x` calendar-market ratio. The balance is consistent with stronger prior-year catalog inventory plus accumulated career/label state. Independent per-record buyer pools amplify that temporary catalog quality difference, as already established by the economic-yield investigation.

The composition is self-limiting. By 1963 the reserve cohort supplies most new decisions, yet the annual economic ratio is already declining; by 1964 it has returned to parity.
