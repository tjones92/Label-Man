# Album and single release practice, 1960-64

Research and calibration, 2026-10-19. US commercial releases are the primary market.
The user's definition governs this investigation: **an album promo is a cut issued before an already
planned LP, expressly to promote that forthcoming LP**. A track's later appearance on an album does
not establish that intention. Existing handoffs are evidence, not instructions.

**Decision:** retain the new `--album-release-practice` behavior as an opt-in research candidate;
do not adopt its current parameters as the balanced bundle. The historical investigation supports
separating the release routes, and all five 260-week candidate runs pass their route/master checks.
However, mean 1960 CR8 is **42.73 versus 44.12** for the reference, below the 48 review floor, and
genre-share error increases in every year. Generic Classical LP cuts fall from 2,169 to zero across
the five seeds. That correction is valuable, but it does not solve the original balance problem.
Gameplay defaults and the reference bundle remain unchanged.

## What the sources establish

The current automatic advance-single pipeline is not a defensible default for this period. The strongest
finding is about the **direction of the decision**, rather than an exact percentage by genre.

Four routes must remain distinct:

1. An independently commissioned single, whether or not it is collected on an LP later.
2. A hit-based LP: existing successful singles plus additional material, sometimes with a unifying theme.
3. An LP followed by a commercial extraction after audience or radio response.
4. A planned LP with a deliberately commissioned advance single.

The third and fourth routes both reuse a recorded master. They have different timing and information.
The second route does not retrospectively turn its earlier singles into route four.
The composition itself may already exist: an advance campaign is identified by its release plan and
purpose, not by whether the songwriter expressly composed the song for that album.

Contemporary reporting and label documentation support routes one through three across adult and youth
markets. This does not prove route four never occurred, or date when the modern launch sequence became
dominant. Those stronger claims require a different longitudinal dataset.

These examples are deliberately selected mechanism cases, **not a probability sample**. Charting hits and
famous artists are overrepresented. There is no defensible national denominator of all new LP programmes,
including failures, paired with dated evidence of advance intent. Therefore no table of exact historical
genre percentages is claimed. Dates and catalogue associations without intent are retained as uncertain.

The original trade-paper corpus is indexed under `SimLogs/album-promo-research/`, with URLs, dates and SHA-256
hashes in `manifest.json`. Twelve successfully indexed issues contain 859 pages; targeted relevant pages
were reviewed, rather than claiming a cover-to-cover reading of all 859. The August 24, 1963 page was also
rendered and visually checked: the headline and

also rendered and checked; Wilson demonstrates why response to earlier LPs must not be mistaken for extracting
one of their masters. Failed downloads are explicit in the

they are not treated as a complete census.

## Calibration specification

`--album-release-practice` is a research candidate layered on the existing hook/writer/TradPop reference.
It does not enable the rejected studio-production experiment. It changes decisions and sequencing, not
genre hooks, demand keyframes, recording execution or chart formulas.

- The physical LP competes on its own component proposition. A speculative future promo cannot create
  LP eligibility before material exists.
- In markets with existing singles orientation at least .6, an act without a previously released single
  usually undertakes a single before committing the LP. The orientation supplies this institutional
  probability; its complement preserves original LP programmes. A prior single programme removes the
  extra hurdle; actual hit inventory still informs the LP forecast. A national pop-chart entry is not
  required, since that would penalize specialist and regional audiences. This is a declared
  behavioural prior, not a measured fraction of historical LPs.
- Only after the LP has been recorded does the label consider its best unissued master for an advance
  campaign. Standard and Concept LPs are eligible; the generic policy does not assume a fresh advance
  campaign for a live or hit-compilation package.
- A selective commissioning prior represents the institutional practice of commissioning a planned LP
  launch campaign. It is not a historical quota or a target measured against simulation output.
- The advance must also beat its incremental costs: master preparation, campaign spending, expected LP
  diversion, the cost of delaying the LP and the independently available singles opportunity.
- LP-only commitments do not automatically erase an act's independently due commercial singles opportunity.
  A separate, bounded label slot enters that act's normal singles path. It can make a new recording and
  pay full costs, select an eligible older LP cut and pay preparation costs, or fail for lack of cash,
  eligible material or a positive single proposition. The just-committed LP is too young for later
  extraction. The slot cannot initiate another LP recursively.
- Later extraction requires an LP already at least eight weeks old, an observed Top 40 LP peak, a
  still-unissued track and a positive direct commercial proposition. It consumes an ordinary singles
  opportunity. One such extraction is allowed per LP in this first model; it has a distinct source LP,
  release role, strategy and estimator lane. No second songwriting or performance realization occurs.
- Existing compilation construction still resolves prior single masters through the live/archive track
  ledger. The new policy does not retrospectively turn those records into advance singles.

### Explicit priors and their limits

The central advance commission probability is `.15 * existing SingleOrientation * institution factor`.
The institution factor is zero for Classical and NonMusic, .25 for Jazz/Gospel, .5 for Blues/Folk, and one
otherwise. That gives these central probabilities **conditional on a new eligible LP being considered**,
before the cost test:

|---|---:|
| Classical / Comedy / Childrens | 0% |
| Jazz | 1.125% |
| Gospel | 2.625% |
| Blues / Folk | 3.75% |
| TraditionalPop | 6.75% |
| EasyListening | 5.25% |
| Country | 9.75% |
| RockAndRoll | 12.75% |
| RnB | 12.00% |
| Soul | 9.90% |

Other canonical genres use the same declared family rule and the existing orientation. There is no
year-by-year curve fitted to noisy 1960-64 chart outcomes and no Major-label multiplier. The same rule
continues after 1964; that continuation is an extrapolation needing separate validation, not historical
research of later decades. `--album-promo-campaign-scale=0.5` and `=2` bracket the commissioning uncertainty.
An experimental zero is also allowed. Rates across **all** LPs will be lower because of format exclusions
and the economic comparison. Rates among **all single launches** have a different denominator again.

These numbers, the eight-week/Top-40/one-cut extraction rule, its .35 opportunity probability, the .15
master-preparation cost fraction and the 10% annual time-cost assumption are modelling choices. The sources
support the distinctions and selectivity, not these exact values. Future work can replace the priors with
a dated, territory-specific release census and replace hard success thresholds with learned expected demand.

The separate slot is a bounded opportunity at LP commitment, not a full studio/session calendar with two
persistent artist clocks. The LP and an independently recorded single may ship in the same week; the normal
artist cooldown then applies. The later-cut rule is intentionally conservative about LP chart success and
will miss some successful niche LPs. Those limitations are reasons to retain the candidate boundary while
evaluating the new release mix, rather than claim a complete historical reconstruction.
The first-single test currently reads resolved singles in the master ledger. A seeded public discography
without reusable masters is a distinct opening-state case; separating known audience from reusable hit
inventory is another requirement before promoting that LP-origination rule to a general default.

### A forecast mismatch discovered in the implementation

The original promo comparison values cannibalization in single units times LP margin, opposed by a large
synthetic recruitment credit and a fixed dollar awareness term. Actual `AlbumSimulator` instead suppresses
a bounded fraction of **LP demand**: `cannibalizationStrength * singleHeat * substitutionPropensity`.
The new candidate forecasts diversion on that LP scale, estimates the actual capped launch-awareness
mechanism, and removes the free dollar term and duplicate recruitment credit. Preparation reuses the master
and pays a fraction of recording cost; a separately recorded single pays full cost. These are structural
economic changes, so the candidate requires macro and genre checks, not just a plausible promo count.

## Experiments and decision

The initial one-year screen (`apr1-*`) is diagnostic only. It exposed two problems: first-week release
decisions preceded the chart clock's live sentinel, and reducing attached promos could remove most of
Soul's singles supply. That version is not the final candidate. Its four runs are retained for causality
and off-boundary evidence. The off run matches all 86 prior CSVs byte for byte.

The completed final comparison and independent opening tests follow. No result is promoted to a
historical percentage simply because it fits the charts.

### What the release mix actually becomes

Across the five seeds and all five years, the reference commits 63,354 artist LP projects and attaches a
commercial advance single to every one. The candidate commits 62,761 LPs, with **1,248 advances (1.99% of
LP commitments)** and **785 later extractions**. It issues 110,509 independent singles. Advances are
1.11% of its 112,542 total single launches, versus 46.08% of 137,480 in the reference. These counts concern
new artist LP projects in this model; they do not describe every album in the simulated market.

The opening-year detail below pools the five seeds (counts are totals, not an annual market census).
The two percentage columns deliberately use different denominators. Every reference LP in the same
comparison has an advance; the candidate's actual rates are lower than its conditional commissioning
priors because formats, costs and opportunity costs can reject a campaign.

For comparison, the reference's 1960 TraditionalPop counts are 1,268 advances and 1,196 independent
singles: 51.46% of its singles launches are attached LP promos, compared with TeenPop's 7.60% and
RockAndRoll's 6.26%. That is the route imbalance the user identified. In the candidate, TraditionalPop
still undertakes substantially more LPs than those youth genres. The available research does not
justify declaring its remaining LP commitment count correct, nor its resulting 2.87% advance rate
historically measured. Classical has 2,623 LP commitments over the full candidate window, with no
generic advance or later cuts; independent crossover propositions remain possible.

The result identifies the next calibration boundary: singles A&R/material selection and LP commitment
economics must be evaluated together. In the old route, a genre issuing many LPs could obtain many
best-of-a-batch singles. Replacing those with ordinary single commissions changes the material distribution
even when sales and artist opportunities survive. Raising the advance prior just to recover Soul's old
share would reintroduce that hidden advantage. This experiment isolates a plausible release system to
develop further; it does not establish a new bundle satisfying the balance guards.

### What the opening tests already identify

On two independent opening seeds, the corrected final policy (`apr6-opening`) keeps mean single sales
at 156.09M versus the reference's 158.79M (-1.70%), and LP sales at 40.83M versus 37.89M (+7.74%).
CR8 is 47.50 versus 46.44; Big-5 share is 34.13 versus 30.48. These two seeds are insufficient to claim
an opening concentration pass. Combined genre-share error rises from 24.53 to 30.65 and slot error from
29 to 37. Soul falls from 6.51% to 4.83%, and TraditionalPop from 14.39% to 12.34%.

The release-route correction is therefore not, by itself, a completed genre-balance bundle. One concrete
source of dependence on the old mechanism is visible in untouched seed 6006: Soul issues 280 LP promos
with mean hook .788, versus 123 orphans at .623. The new same-seed route issues 358 independent singles
at .595, eleven advance cuts at .761, and one later extraction at .844. The change includes cohort and
equilibrium responses; these are not matched-record treatment effects. The code does establish the
selection difference: an LP records a batch of tracks and the extraction chooses its best candidate.
Restoring the old number of advance singles would conceal that difference in A&R and material selection.

The low/high final commissioning scenarios produce 0.90%/3.47% advances among all LP commitments on seed
6006, versus 2.04% centrally. Those are uncertainty experiments, not historical estimates. All four final
opening runs pass the chronology, recording/master identity and forecast-component checks; Classical,
Comedy and Childrens mint no generic advance or later LP cuts, including in week one.

An intermediate rule (`apr4`/`apr5`) incorrectly used national pop-chart entry as the only prior-single
evidence for LP formation. It was discarded before the final five-year comparison; four partially begun
`apr5-main` runs were deliberately stopped and are not valid results. The final rule uses a prior resolved
commercial single, permits original LP programmes, and leaves actual hit inventory in the economic prior.

The final zero-advance uncertainty case (`apr6-zero-6006-52`) completes normally: zero advance cuts,
41 later extractions, 155.27M single units and 40.63M LP units. The same-seed reference is 160.00M and
35.78M. This shows that advance singles are not necessary to sustain **opening-year** sales in the repaired
opportunity model. It is not a claim about later decades or evidence that the historical rate was exactly zero.

The final disabled replay matches all 86 reference CSVs byte for byte, including the prior read-only genre
audits. The 36 C# checks pass. Every completed final-policy run is checked against actual single launches
for master ID, composition ID, hook and production equality; later cuts retain their source LP and satisfy
the observed-age/peak conditions. Each advance forecast reconciles its cost and benefit components.
One six-decimal audit row rounded a valid roll up to its threshold; reconstructing its exact FNV draw gave
0.05249953269958496, below .0525. The validator records that evidence rather than waiving the condition.
