## Timing and initial sites

* `ChartManager.OnWeekEnded` settles and books the completed market week before `MediaAtmosphereService.AfterCompletedWeek`. Inside the latter, `FollowingWeek` precedes `AiPlanWeek`; a standing snapshot intended for those decisions must be published after following and before planning. The entire media method currently returns early when media observation is disabled, so the eventual observation switch must define its own availability and fallback explicitly.
* `FollowingWeek` builds weekly regional buyer and fresh TV registration arrays locally, then discards them. Those TV values are registration estimates that feed following, not automatically retained fan joins. The observation path needs an explicit, read-only handoff of the appropriate response, with format separated for sales, and should identify any estimates as such. It must avoid reading `Salience()` or national episode labels as inputs to standing.
* `AILabel.GetMarketingBudget` is used by single and album planning, release-programme calculations, tranche spending, media envelopes, projections and acquisition-related paths. It is not a single release-only decision. Audit ownership and timing at each call before a marketing screen.
* `CalculateAlbumPriorNet` reads `GetAlbumPriorCareerMultiplier`. Besides the format decision, programme projections and LP assembly pricing call it, potentially repeatedly for alternatives. An observation at this shared method alone would count calculations rather than necessarily distinct decisions. Identify the decision context and snapshot timing before choosing the reader boundary.
* `GetCareerBandIndex` also selects historical format memory; it is a distinct candidate from the album prior. `ApplyReleasePromotion` and the deferred `ApplyPromotionSnapshot` affect stock using the career fallback when recognition is disabled; recognition-enabled paths use recognition instead. Inspect deferred snapshots alongside launch-time state before changing either path.
* `ContractPricing` reads the greater of current and before-drop career values. `RosterManager.ShouldResignArtist` protects Star and above. Contract pricing, renewal and player-owned paths require separate scope and fixtures.
* `SimulatedArtist` changes career state through its transition logic; `SeniorCooling` writes it separately. `RosterManager` initializes opening and signed careers; `ArtistManager` handles signing and terminal states; `AILabel` can drop an artist. This is not yet an exhaustive writer inventory.

No game state, RNG, episode engine or AI decision has been changed in this initial step.

## Raw weekly evidence slice

`--observe-stature-evidence` in `ChartAuditRunner` requires active media following and creates a separate `stature-evidence.csv`. After following churn and the week's joins, it writes one row per AI-label roster artist and market region for each completed week, including quiet weeks. It reads the same record regional weekly sales used by following, including off-chart records, separated by format. The young/adult split applies the existing genre-and-format buyer-share model; it is not observed individual buyer demographics. The corresponding regional format/age market totals include all records, including player records and records without an artist ID. Region populations are in millions.

TV columns are the existing fresh-viewer registration estimates for ordinary appearances, not confirmed retained followers or an airing's raw reach. Resident-series join offers and live/press joins are not in those TV columns. Following is post-churn and post-join; `no_following_record` distinguishes an absent/pruned record from an existing one. The intent columns interpret each region's following using the configured follower rates and single scale; they are not incremental sales, stocked units, or national/reachable totals. Recognition is diagnostic only. The CSV includes no standing score, bands, normalization, reference distribution, smoothing or decision-time rows; unsigned acts and artists no longer on an AI label are outside this initial roster snapshot. It does not persist a standing snapshot, record actual TV joins by region, or establish route coverage. These are remaining work, not zero evidence.

`run_stature_observation_smoke.ps1` runs same-assembly control and observation arms, checks completion and error logs, and compares all common CSV outputs byte for byte. `verify_stature_evidence.py` checks uniqueness, nonnegative values, consistent regional denominators, AI sales bounds and follower-intent arithmetic. A two-week smoke comparison exercises the instrumentation, not the trajectory or full-decade evidence gates.

The `stature-evidence-v2` smoke pairs completed for both seeds with empty error logs. All 143 common CSVs matched byte for byte for each seed; the sole added CSV was the evidence trace. Integrity checks passed for 41,958 rows (2,997 artists) in seed 1001 and 41,944 rows (2,996 artists) in seed 2002. ## Offline normalization comparison

`stature_normalization.py` compares two experimental references for each separate single/album and young/adult sales share, ordinary-TV registration estimate, and young/adult following. It smooths each weekly response once with an explicitly supplied half-life; following is read after its existing churn without extra smoothing. The lagged reference is the mean of up to N highest values from the previous observed week, after removing the evaluated artist, with artist-ID tie ordering, an explicit minimum peer population and component-specific denominator floors. The fixed reference and floors must be supplied in a versioned JSON file with an effective date; the tool does not apply that reference before its date. The user of the tool must establish that the fixed distribution was frozen independently and contains no future information. Both scores are clipped to [0,1].

An absent following record and a zero market denominator are missing, not zero; genuine zero response is smoothed as zero. A missing week or an act absent in the prior week resets its smoothing history and the reference is unavailable across a missing week. This deliberately avoids fabricating quiet weeks where roster coverage is unknown, but it does not yet provide the continuous off-roster clock or save/resume snapshot required by the directive. Only AI-label roster acts enter the peer distribution. The component output has no reach weights, route aggregation, calibrated bands, decision-time context or cohort classification; cohort is explicitly `unavailable`. It is an offline comparison, not a standing model or an AI reader.

`StatureNormalizationExample.json` contains invented values for plumbing tests only. A two-week smoke used those same invented values (half-life 4 weeks, top 20, minimum 25 peers), with the two completed `stature-evidence-v2` traces. Both runs completed; seed 1001 produced 47,952 component rows, including 10,436 explicitly missing following components, and the first week's 23,976 rows correctly had no lagged reference. `summarize_stature_normalization.py` creates year/cohort/tier/genre/component distributions, but the available two weeks and unknown cohorts cannot calibrate a policy or support drift and trajectory conclusions. The parameter choices are not recommendations.

The normalization fixtures cover no future or self reference, ties, denominator floors, clipping, insufficient peers, zero response, missing following and markets, week gaps and incomplete regional coverage. Longer traces, independent fixed references, opening-versus-new cohort data, reach and decision-site observations remain necessary before fitting or selecting a policy.

## Experimental band-policy kernel

`stature_shadow_policy.py` provides a standalone offline policy kernel, not a simulation reader or an analysis of the recorded traces. Callers supply normalized recent response, durable draw and reach separately for regional, record and conversion routes, plus a dated, versioned calibration. Each route takes the maximum of response and draw, multiplies it by a continuous band-specific reach weight, and competes with the other routes. Bands are evaluated from Superstar down using entry or exit boundaries according to the previous band. Validation requires higher bands to have no lower boundaries or reach weights. The kernel returns per-route support bounds and distinguishes below Rising from an indeterminate result when missing evidence could change the selected band. Its caller must retain the previous band only after a determinate result and handle observation gaps explicitly.

The test values in `test_stature_shadow_policy.py` are invented and are not a proposed calibration. Fixtures exercise jumps, hysteresis, cooling, quiet weeks, route competition, continuous reach, missing inputs and invalid calibration. The kernel does not yet derive regional reach, assemble routes from the trace, maintain weekly state, publish snapshots, fit a calibration, or establish natural trajectories. In particular, the TV estimate remains a proxy and no causal reader is authorized by these fixtures.

`stature_shadow_trace.py` connects the normalized component output to the kernel with a separately supplied reach CSV. It conservatively leaves the regional route unknown because regional-response normalization is not implemented, carries a determinate prior band through an unknown week, and resets hysteresis across a missing week. As a plumbing check, the two-week seed 1001 component trace, invented fixture calibration and empty reach file produced 5,994 indeterminate observations. This is not a rank distribution or evidence for a policy. The adapter's use of a shared following proxy across record and conversion routes also needs a format-specific design before calibration.

## Regional and reader preparation

`stature_regional_evidence.py` is an experimental offline measurement: it smooths each region's format/age sales share, compares local strength against an explicitly supplied fixed reference, and weights strength by regional population. Following density is read after churn, without another smoother; record/regional reach uses the local maximum of sales and following strength, while conversion reach uses following density. Missing markets and missing following records remain unknown. National format-specific advance intents are emitted as interpretations of following, not added as extra demand. A regional sales floor, density reference and half-life are experimental inputs, not calibrated findings. This proxy still needs validation against actual local audience conversion and meaningful-market definitions.

The first-week fixed-reference candidate was fit on one SHA-256 artist-ID partition and assessed on the other, effective one week later. Its 95th percentile fell to the denominator floors for both album and TV components. On held-out seed 1001 observations, every nonzero album and TV score clipped to one; the lagged top-20 reference clipped only about 0.2–0.4% of all observations. A ten-week positive-observation percentile candidate reduced that clipping, but fixed and lagged following scores remained materially different. These short exploratory comparisons do not choose a reference or bands. `fit_stature_window.py` adds a prior-window, nonzero-percentile alternative with minimum sample counts and a recorded training/evaluation split; it is explicitly based on raw weekly values even though the response being normalized is smoothed.

The album-prior calculation is shared by release-format decisions, programme candidate ranking, LP preparation/assembly alternatives, and production-cost estimation. A switch at the shared function would change several contexts at once. The programme path can force the eventual format after `DecideRelease`, and LP assembly can replace its forecast with separate alternative pricing. Therefore a causal arm needs an explicit decision boundary and a shared dated snapshot across the affected forecast and commitment; simply replacing the multiplier globally is not a single bounded test. The existing `--label-decision-audit` captures selected, passed-over and ineligible programme candidates. A four-week seed-1001 same-build control/observation check with this flag matched 144 common CSVs and recorded 25,614 candidate rows, including 119 eligible album candidates and 12,122 ineligible album candidates. It does not yet capture every required decision input or prove a calibrated mapping.

### One-year exploratory screen

The `stature-year-v2` 52-week control/observation pairs for seeds 1001 and 2002 both completed with empty stderr and 143 common CSVs matching byte for byte. The validators accepted 1,062,320 rows for 3,266 artists in 1001 and 1,068,809 rows for 3,287 artists in 2002. The same source/build and configuration limitations recorded above apply. The first 26 weeks of seed 1001 fit a fixed reference on the training artist partition, with the remaining weeks and all complementary artist IDs held out. The reference becomes effective July 1, 1960. `stature-fit-26w-v2` retains regional 90th- and 99th-percentile alternatives. Neither represents a policy decision.

A first fit mapping each legacy rung's median support to its band collapsed under the 90th-percentile regional reference: all ordered entry boundaries reached 1. Under the 99th-percentile reference, Rising's training median exceeded Established's, so the ordered boundaries still overlapped. `fit_stature_bands.py` rejects overlapping boundaries rather than emitting a runnable mapping. A second, explicitly experimental mapping uses frozen pooled support quantiles (50/75/90/97%), band reach weights (0/.25/.5/.75) and exit boundaries at 80% of entry. These are research assumptions, not a selected mapping or per-week quotas. It fitted 7,275 complete training artist-weeks. The complete-case training population excludes absent-following records and may be biased.

On held-out July–December artist-weeks, the pooled candidate differed from the legacy rung on 1,702 of 2,806 comparable observations in seed 1001 (60.7%) and 1,644 of 2,709 in seed 2002 (60.7%). These are weekly observations, not distinct acts or decisions, and comparable rows exclude lifecycle states outside the four bands. In this active-following, post-update trace, inspection of `FollowingWeek` establishes that discarded states contain fewer than 25 followers. An opt-in experimental bound of 0–25 followers, propagated conservatively through reference and reach bounds, resolves those observations as below Rising in both runs. It does not claim past following was zero, and must not be applied to older saves or observation-disabled runs without checking their initialization and availability. The two modes, per-band bounds, cross-tabs and route counts are retained in the `stature-shadow-*` outputs. No candidate has been tested as a live reader.

The 52-week `stature-choice-year-v1` audit pairs matched 144 common files for both seeds; their controls also matched all 143 common CSVs from the unaudited year runs. For NewSigning acts below Rising it preserves the existing NewSigning table value; other below-Rising and nonstandard lifecycle cases are kept unresolved. `stature-sample-evidence-v1` contains descriptive samples selected for rises, declines, reversals and leading routes, with weekly sales, following and estimated TV registration. Route leadership can reflect durable draw as well as the named response, so it does not by itself prove an LP-led or conversion-led breakthrough. These chart entries are not a complete inventory of the legacy ladder's milestones. The window, cohorts, exposure/spend attribution and full trajectories remain inadequate for adoption.

The game-side `StatureShadowPolicy` and `StatureShadowLedger` are pure, opt-in building blocks with no simulation integration. The ledger stores a dated prior band and calibration version, rejects out-of-order/gapped weeks, carries hysteresis through an explicitly missing result, and prepares a whole update before publishing it. A round-trip fixture exercises its serialization and idempotence; it is not yet part of the game save schema, does not collect or smooth evidence, and must not be treated as the required live snapshot or reader. A snapshot for an artist omitted from a weekly update is stale and a future reader must check its observation date.

The selected “comeback” examples are short reversals, not established career-long comebacks.

`StaturePeerNormalizer` is an isolated game-side implementation of the lagged top-N comparison. It accepts externally measured components and caller-supplied, versioned parameters; it smooths response components once, takes post-churn draw directly, excludes the evaluated artist, uses ordinal tie ordering and preserves missing values. The same-build two-week control/observation pairs for both seeds on the normalizer assembly matched all 143 common CSVs; since the new kernel and normalizer are not wired into the observation path, that comparison confirms disabled-mode neutrality only, not neutrality of a live shadow model.

## Live shadow pilot

The prior paragraph describes the earlier isolated stage. The audit runner now enables a weekly shadow with `--observe-stature-shadow=<calibration.json>` and `--label-decision-audit`. It runs after `FollowingWeek`, using realized regional single/LP sales, fresh-viewer registration estimates and post-churn following. The supplied `StatureShadowExperimental.json` is the previously screened experimental candidate, not an adopted policy. Its contents are hashed into the saved normalization and policy versions; a changed calibration cannot silently resume an existing shadow. The first 26 weeks warm the measured histories without publishing a calibrated band; the experimental policy becomes effective July 1, 1960. This is an observation stream and no AI decision reads it.

Unsigned AI acts continue accumulating evidence and regional history, but only AI-roster acts enter the prior-week peer distribution. Thus an unsigned spell and re-signing can carry measured audience forward without changing the roster reference population. Player-owned acts and labels are excluded. Quiet weeks supply zero sales; genuinely missing markets and missing following records remain unknown in the live shadow. Unlike the separate offline bounded sensitivity, this live implementation does not yet apply the 0–25 pruned-following bound. The snapshot records its date, components, peer references, route reach and per-band support. Stale snapshots remain dated rather than becoming current observations.

`StatureDecisionSnapshot.Resolve` is an observational decision-time resolver. It accepts only an earlier completed-week snapshot no older than seven days, keeps player-owned and terminal lifecycle states authoritative, preserves NewSigning when below Rising, and falls back to the current rung for missing, stale, declining, dropped and other unresolved lifecycle contexts. It never writes `careerState` or applies the proposed multiplier. Programme album-candidate audit rows record the old, proposed and actually applied multiplier, current and before-drop states, label cash, eligibility and snapshot date. This is one inventoried candidate context; format forks and LP assembly remain separate, and the audit does not yet contain every capacity, commitment, cohort or attribution field required for a causal screen.

Normal shadow observation is transient and omits the new field from saves. The separate explicit `--persist-stature-shadow` switch allows a save/resume test; older saves without it cold-start the shadow rather than fabricating prior evidence. A 28-week full-world round trip passed with the opt-in shadow present, with a byte-identical capture/rehydration and a next-week clock assertion. The focused fixture covers date ordering, idempotence, serialization, unsigned/re-signed continuity, player ownership, terminal states and missing evidence. This does not establish longer absence behavior when ownership itself is unavailable, nor does it authorize a causal reader.

In the 28-week `stature-live-screen-v2` paired simulations, both seeds matched all 144 common CSVs byte for byte, with empty error logs. Seed 1001's 5,826 comparable post-calibration live and offline bands matched; six component and 40 support values differed for two acts with off-roster gaps, because the live implementation deliberately retains evidence through those gaps while the earlier offline comparison reset it. ### Save/resume continuation check

The round-trip runner now accepts `--stature-replay-weeks=N` with `--stature-live-roundtrip=<calibration>`. It captures a world, advances N weeks, restores the captured date and serialized world, verifies capture/rehydration identity, then advances N weeks again and compares the complete serialized shadow. This compares live-world continuations, so differing inputs from the wider world can also cause a mismatch; it does not isolate a shadow defect.

With the recorded live-smoke profile and seed 1001, a 28-week capture followed by a two-week replay failed the shadow comparison: the uninterrupted and resumed serializations first differed at character 792 and had lengths 24,714,743 and 24,753,534. Both continuations reached the same dates, and the initial capture and rehydration matched. The cause has not been isolated. This failed check supersedes any inference that the earlier one-week clock assertion establishes replay parity. No causal reader is authorized by this experiment.

An opt-in replay-only observer now compares the inputs to the shadow before comparing its output, and reports the first differing artist's regional sales, individual record sales and regional market totals. In a one-week continuation from the same 28-week seed-1001 capture, the first differing component was `artist_00005`'s young single-sales share: 0.00004193392977125182 uninterrupted versus 0.00006462247111796131 resumed. The Deep South contribution to that artist's young single sales was 5.30 versus 7.42 modeled units, corresponding to record `gen_1792`'s 10 versus 14 cleared units and the existing buyer-share split. The Deep South young single market total also differed: 129,408.51 versus 130,929.12. Other regions and records differed as well; the detailed results are in `SimLogs/writing-calibration/stature-replay-trace-v5.log`. These are post-clearing world inputs, so this locates the immediate source of the differing shadow value outside normalization but does not explain why market clearing diverged after restoration or prove parity when supplied identical inputs. The observer is inactive outside the explicit replay check.

The world loader deliberately reseeds the global RNG on restore. A replay-only experiment restored its captured state after loading; this did not eliminate the discrepancy. In that experiment `gen_1792`'s Deep South serviceable intent was 90 uninterrupted versus 92 resumed, and its cleared allocation was 10 versus 14. The region's singles budget was 190,806 in both arms, but total serviceable singles intent was 1,683,195 versus 1,236,847, so the record competed for the same budget against substantially different total demand. The largest individual raw-demand changes include `gen_2171` (253,008 versus 52,263) and `gen_2005` (312,169 versus 114,330); supply constraints mean raw-demand changes do not translate one-for-one to serviceable intent. See `stature-demand-trace-v4.log` for the targeted and population traces. The RNG states had diverged again by the demand pass despite the test-only reset, and the underlying causes of the competing-demand changes remain unproven. This is an in-process rollback experiment, not a comparison of the baseline build against a stature treatment or proof about fresh-process saves. Earlier same-build disabled/shadow CSV comparisons remain the evidence for ordinary observation neutrality.

The runner's `--stature-evidence-only-replay` mode isolates the shadow from that world divergence. It captures a serialized world at week 28, records the exact evidence supplied to the uninterrupted shadow in each subsequent week, restores the shadow from the captured world in memory, and feeds it those same dated observations. It compares the entire serialized shadow after every week and checks duplicate-week idempotence. With the recorded live-smoke profile and experimental calibration, seeds 1001 and 2002 each passed three replayed weeks after the July 8, 1960 checkpoint (`stature-evidence-replay-v1-1001.log` and `-2002.log`). An additional synthetic fixture compares complete states over quiet, missing-following and off-roster observations. This demonstrates continuation for these recorded inputs and fixtures, not full-world save/load parity, broader trajectory coverage, or reader readiness. The replay and its evidence collection are opt-in test paths.

### Fresh-process continuation gate

`run_stature_fresh_continuation.ps1` runs a recording process to the selected checkpoint, writes the AI world through the save serializer to a compressed file, and records dated evidence plus the complete serialized shadow separately after every continuation week. It waits for that process to exit, then starts a separate Godot process with separate user data and loads the checkpoint over its freshly generated world. The checkpoint records build, engine, calibration, seed, exact user flags and producer PID. The consumer requires a different PID, matching configuration, verified artifact hashes, byte-identical initial world rehydration, and identical evidence and complete shadow serialization for every week. A ready receipt is written only after the full reference continuation completes. Files are created exclusively. The extra `-TraceWorld` option compares saved world sections at the first and final continuation week; its differences remain distinct from the shadow pass condition. This harness exercises fresh-process AI-world checkpoint continuity, not restoration of a player's separate desk layer.

The earlier album-project discrepancies were substantive save-graph problems. `generatedProjectCounter` was not restored, allowing a fresh process to reuse IDs and an in-process rollback to retain future IDs. Pending projects were deserialized twice, so completing the pending copy did not update its history/index object. The loader also relinked every historical project record to a live record, even when the uninterrupted world deliberately held separate objects. The opt-in continuation section now stores the counter and the actual live-record alias flags. Restoring it joins pending entries to their canonical history objects and only relinks record references that were shared when captured. Ordinary save behavior is unchanged when this experimental section is absent. These relationships affect project lookup and outcome-memory paths, so they cannot be dismissed as display-only IDs or roles.
