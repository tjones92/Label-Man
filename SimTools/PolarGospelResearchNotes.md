# Gospel research notes — Directive 3

Pre-registration dated October 5, 2026. Written before new variant runs or source edits. Evidence is about historical repertoire, not a calibration to seeds 1001/1002. No lyrics reproduced. Unsigned does not mean church choir, and recordings do not establish live-set proportions.

## R1. Set composition

No representative 1960 or 1963 live-program survey dividing songs into the four requested authorship categories was located. Consequently no defensible numerical split is available for any of the four subgenres. Do not substitute LP track counts for live-set shares.

Black quartets: the Library of Congress documents the Soul Stirrers and Cooke's transition from their religious repertoire into pop [S1]. This establishes a professional sacred performance tradition, not the fraction written by the group. Black choirs/ensembles/soloists: Smithsonian's contemporary field recordings document congregational songs, hymns and gospel alongside soloists and choirs [S2]. Southern quartets/families: publication, singing schools and publisher quartets promoted both existing and newly composed religious songs [S3]. Local church/community: Hymnary's catalog of a 1956 Baptist hymnal demonstrates a substantial available worship collection [S4], but access to a hymnal is not an act's active repertoire.

Inference: inherited sacred material is plausible in all four settings; the stronger premise that most professional sets were traditional spirituals is not established. Composed gospel and new house material must remain distinct. Secular set percentages are unknown. For each category the only empirical numerical bound from this pass is 0–100%, subject to shares summing to 100%; this is deliberately not an actionable band.

## R2. Scale and act definition

No nationwide deduplicated count of active professional, regional, or local gospel groups for January 1960, September 1961, or 1963 was found. A publisher's songbook sales, church membership, singers, and recording credits cannot be counted as distinct acts. Historical ranges at all three tiers therefore remain unknown; none is sized from the simulation.

The simulation act is a stable `SimulatedArtist.artistId` in a label roster or the unsigned artist pool, potentially with multiple members, producing releases and available for a scouting live set. For comparison the signed pool is a commercial recording tier; unsigned combines aspirants/regional performers rather than representing every US church choir. This mapping is an interpretation of source behavior, not a historical census. D1 counts distinct roster/pool identities separately from sampled or zero-slot identities. No population rule is authorized to change.

## R3. Canon, working repertoire and concentration

[S4] indexes the 1956 Baptist Hymnal through item 554. This supports a printed sacred collection larger than 350, but not 554 distinct commonly performed gospel compositions, and not equal access across Black quartet, choir, Southern and local traditions. [S3] describes publishers releasing new songbooks regularly and distributing them through conventions; this supports continued circulation of new composed material. Neither source measures a group's working repertoire or overlap among groups. Their available collection, shared canon, and reachable simulation pool must not be conflated.

**Phase 3 historical gate remains unestablished:** this pass cannot verify more than 350 commonly circulating, reachable standards across the simulated Gospel act definition. Proposed expansion range is withheld rather than filled with synthetic historic titles or a target fitted to dose-response results. D2 remains useful to test the separate software access gate. If Alice accepts a printed-library abstraction, a later append-only proposal can define a corpus and its subgenre mix before expansion runs.

## R4. Crossover

[S1] documents Cooke's gospel career and 1957 pop success. The UCLA dissertation [S5] specifically discusses the 1956 secular remake released as Dale Cook and the tension surrounding that transition. This is evidence of a career boundary and commercial/social friction, not evidence that active gospel quartets habitually inserted secular songs into worship programs. [S3] documents country/bluegrass use of religious material; that reverse direction is distinct. Gospel-derived musical style or a secular adaptation does not establish that a composition's subject is sacred. Unknown context remains unknown in D5; no selector context term is proposed as a production repair.

## R5. 1960–1963 trajectory

[S6] documents spiritual/gospel and other sources of civil-rights freedom songs during 1960–1966. It supports a change in venues and uses, not a numerical change in commercial Gospel live sets over exactly 1960–1963. No yearly group growth rate, inherited-share trend, or change in common canon was found. No interpolation of the simulation ramp into historical evidence is permitted.
