# Does the combined profile cause natural small-label turnover?

2026-11-03. Follow-up to `WritingCombinedIntegrationScreen.md`, prompted by the
user's suggestion that additional closures may reflect stronger competition.
This investigation reads the existing completed worlds; no simulation rules,
fees, reserves, mortality thresholds or gameplay defaults change.

## Finding

The evidence is consistent with increased commercial turnover among smaller
labels. It does **not** justify treating every additional closure as a defect
or rescuing firms to restore the control's survival rate. Among opening label
identities that survive only in the control, the combined world usually has
lower sales and receipts and fewer new releases before closure. Most cash
spending falls rather than rises. There is no recorded single-capital-risk
refusal in those firms' final 12-week windows.

This does not identify how many exits were *caused by competition*. The broad
arm changes repertoire, audience allocation, LP assembly, forecasts, costs and
release rules simultaneously. Lower receipts can be downstream of losing buyers,
not releasing new records, changing format/margin mix or collection timing.
An observed cash shortfall can be a consequence of decline, a constraint on
recovery, or both. The next diagnosis should explain why releases stop before
assuming the financial rules need relief.

## Populations and exit reasons

The endpoint counts remain 162 → 232 closed labels across seeds 1001 and 2002:

| Classification | Control | Combined | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bankruptcy | 137 | 185 | +48 |
| Solvent founder dormancy | 25 | 47 | +22 |
| Small at exit | 33 | 69 | +36 |
| Independent at exit | 77 | 103 | +26 |
| Boutique at exit | 29 | 40 | +11 |
| MidTier at exit | 23 | 20 | −3 |
| Major at exit | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Reasons and tiers classify the same exits and are not additive. Thus 22 of the
70 net additional exits are dormancy. The other 48 are bankruptcies, but a
bankruptcy can still be a coherent result of commercial competition. The code
uses two consecutive negative cash month-ends; founder dormancy is a separate
solvent retirement decision with its existing quiet-period and funding guards.

We then match the 596 opening label identities in each seed using name,
archetype, historical flag and **initial** tier. Duplicate or differing identity
sets are rejected. There are 1,192 label-world identities, not 1,192 independent
historical firms:

| Endpoint relationship | 1001 | 2002 | Pooled |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Control survives, combined closes | 77 | 70 | **147** |
| Control closes, combined survives | 43 | 37 | **80** |
| Both close | 41 | 32 | 73 |
| Both survive | 435 | 457 | 892 |

The opening cohort therefore accounts for a net 67 extra closures; later-observed
firms account for the other 3. Initial-tier opening exits rise Small 32 → 69,
Independent 69 → 91 and Boutique 29 → 40, while MidTier falls 23 → 20.
This avoids mistaking a later demotion for an initially Small business.

The 147 additional-closing opening identities comprise 114 bankruptcies and 33
dormancies. These **gross** identity changes must not be conflated with the net
70 additional exits across all firms. Another 80 opening identities survive in
the combined world when they close in the control. Across all observed firms,
historical-label closures fall 17 → 16 while procedural-label closures rise
145 → 216.

## What happens before the 147 additional closures?

For each identity, the comparison ends at the earliest exit in either world,
or week 104 for survivors. Sales/release/refusal observations use the same last
12 calendar weeks in both worlds. Cash bridges use the same final three common
closed calendar-month statements. All 147 additional-closing identities have
three common months; 146 have identical initial statement cash to the cent.
Matching an identity does not freeze its roster, catalogue or world trajectory.

| Pre-exit observation for the 147 identities | Count |
|---|---:|
| Lower label-sales units in combined | **129 (87.8%)** |
| Lower collected record receipts | **136 (92.5%)** |
| Lower total observed new releases | 124 (84.4%) |
| Lower net cash movement over the three common months | 128 |
| Record receipts are largest negative cash-bridge component | **131 (89.1%)** |
| A recorded programme cash shortfall in final 12 weeks | 134 |
| A recorded capital-risk refusal in final 12 weeks | **0** |

Label-sales units total 6,436,512 → 3,121,167 across these firm-specific windows;
the median paired change is −16,096 units. These windows end at different calendar
weeks across firms, so this is a cohort description, not a national annual
market-share estimate or an identified transfer to particular winners.

The LP route is particularly different:

| Output in these pre-exit windows | Control | Combined |
|---|---:|---:|
| Single units | 4,836,788 | 3,004,886 |
| Album units | **1,599,724** | **116,281** |
| New single launches | 201 | 12 |
| Released untransferred album projects | **159** | **4** |
| Identities issuing an untransferred album | 96 | 4 |

Album units decline for 122 of the 147 identities. This makes LP availability,
commitment and economics a sharper mechanism to investigate than a blanket
single-risk or writer-fee explanation. A fall in cash receipts also need not be
proportional to a fall in units: losing higher-priced LP business changes format
mix, and receipts also depend on contracts, margins and collection timing.

The pattern is not simply weaker opening cash or a universal initial sales
disadvantage: only 48 of the 147 have lower sales in the first four weeks, and
none has a recorded programme cash shortfall in those first four weeks. This
observer covers refused work-budget reservations, not every possible initial
funding or eligibility gate.

### Reconciled cash bridge

Every company-month row is checked against its cash identity: opening cash plus
record receipts, service income, signed financing and owner/parent transfers,
minus plant payments, recording, campaigns, advances, licensing, operating and
commercial costs, staff/premises/retainers, taxes and capital spending, plus
rounding equals closing cash within one cent. Accrual earnings, receivables and
inventory changes are not added again as spendable cash.

Across the 147 firms' three-month windows, combined minus control:

| Cash component | Change in cash movement |
|---|---:|
| Collected record receipts | **−$7.156M** |
| Service income | −$0.131M |
| Plant payments | +$1.437M (less spending) |
| Recording | +$0.407M (less spending) |
| Campaigns | +$0.054M (less spending) |
| Distribution operations, trade support and excise | +$1.601M (less spending) |
| Licensing | −$0.032M (more spending) |
| All recorded cash components together | **−$3.121M** |

These are accounting contributions, not estimated policy effects. In particular,
there is no broad pre-exit recording/campaign/plant-spend explosion in this
cohort. Licensing is not the dominant observed negative cash component. Lower
commercial and manufacturing bills can themselves follow reduced sales.

### Release drought and the funding feedback loop

In the final 12 weeks, **138/147** additional-closing identities issue no new
single, compared with 46/147 of their control counterparts. Only nine have new
singles in both worlds during these windows; five have a lower average hook in
the combined world, with median paired hook change −.009. That small, selected
subset cannot establish a general quality decline. The major observable feature
is the absence of new releases, not a measured quality deficit among issued ones.

There are 3,662 deduplicated cash-shortfall slot/weeks in the combined windows,
versus 471 in the controls. In 788 combined observations, current cash covers
the requested production, marketing and pressing amounts but not those amounts
plus protected reserves and other commitments. That is not evidence the holds
are erroneous: they protect real obligations and continued operation. The other
2,874 observations lack even that immediate project cash. Refusal events can
recur and a plan may later issue; these are not 3,662 identified lost releases.

Only 30 funded single-risk reviews are reached in these combined windows,
and all commit. This weakens the specific hypothesis that late capital-risk
utility rejection directly explains this cohort's drought. It does not rule out
earlier decisions, changed priors, all-in profitability, material judgement,
staffing, roster changes, or cash reserves before the risk evaluator is reached.

Counts of new singles use actual launch label IDs. Album release counts include
only released projects without ownership transfer, assigned to their original
label; transferred projects are omitted rather than attributed to the wrong
firm. The new-release count comparison therefore has this explicit album scope.

## The 80 identities that instead survive

The reverse group matters when judging the configuration. In the same windows
ending at their control-world closure, only 8/80 have lower sales and 7/80 lower
receipts in the combined world. Units rise 2,199,864 → 7,663,758, and record
receipts rise $7.934M. Net cash movement rises $3.080M despite larger production
and operating expenditures. Seventy-eight have no new control single in the
window, versus eighteen in the combined world.

Their untransferred album releases rise **2 → 93**, with issuing identities
2 → 56; album units rise **82,662 → 1,607,989**. New singles rise 2 → 141.
The opposite LP outcomes in these two cohorts are stronger descriptive evidence
of redistributed viable release programmes than the net closure count alone.

This looks like commercial success and failure being redistributed across
firms, consistent with the user's selection hypothesis. It does not establish
that these eighty companies acquired the other group's exact artists, songs or
customers. Their observation windows differ, and the broad configuration changes
many mechanisms and random trajectories at once.

## Recommendation

Keep the combined profile as the investigation baseline and treat closure
composition as an outcome, rather than setting the control's survival count as
a target. The immediate follow-up should trace **the onset of release drought**:
which plans/material/financial gates stop issuing before receipts and liquidity
collapse, and whether a legitimate existing LP master could satisfy a due single
without an unnecessary new session. That retains competitive pressure and the
existing cash obligations while testing a concrete allocation gap.

For separating competition from changed economics, the next small matched
comparison should hold writing/media/retail settings fixed and vary a clearly
defined **LP commitment/assembly/cost** configuration. The broad arm includes
corrections to new versus reused recording costs as well as LP assembly and
release practice. It may be removing easy profits from the older compilation
route, changing which actual LP projects clear the commitment gate, or both;
the present comparison does not identify that causal path. A corresponding
repertoire/opportunity comparison can then hold those economics fixed. These
comparisons diagnose mechanisms without declaring that firms should be saved.
Correct physical-inventory/channel
integration before using a literal all-on configuration for the branch-end
decade comparison.

## Reproduction and checks

```powershell
python SimTools/analyze_combined_label_turnover.py
python -m unittest discover -s SimTools -p test_combined_label_turnover.py
```

The analyzer validates 104-week completion, clean error logs and a common
assembly, rejects ambiguous opening identities, reconciles all company cash
rows, intersects calendar months and deduplicates slot/week reviews. Six fixtures
pass, covering signed cash movements/rounding, unexplained money, same-calendar
month selection, earlier-exit censoring, review deduplication and the distinction
between Bankrupt and Defunct snapshots. Generated firm-level bridges and cohort
summaries are under `SimLogs/writing-calibration/writing-combined-label-turnover.json`.
